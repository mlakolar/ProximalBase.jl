Coordinate descent

Assumes: derivatives, quadratic upper models and KKT conditions · You will learn: scalar and block updates, residual maintenance and working-set screening.

Coordinate methods update one entry or one ordered block at a time. A working set is the subset selected for updates and screening; it is not necessarily the nonzero support. The caller constructs a trial, requests a pure proximal result, writes the result and updates its residual/cache. ProximalBase supplies the function and storage operations; the schedule and curvature calculations below are caller code.

A scalar coordinate update

For $F(x)=f(x)+\sum_jg_j(x_j)$, suppose $a_j>0$ satisfies

\[f(x+(t-x_j)e_j)\le f(x)+\nabla_j f(x)(t-x_j)+\frac{a_j}{2}(t-x_j)^2.\]

Completing the square in the model gives

\[x_j^+=\operatorname{prox}_{g_j/a_j} \left(x_j-\frac{\nabla_j f(x)}{a_j}\right).\]

The scalar call is prox_block(g,trial,j,1/a[j]). It accepts a numerical trial and the global entry index, and does not mutate storage. ProximalBase does not provide a coordinate-curvature helper; compute a valid curvature from the model. Handle a zero curvature explicitly before inversion.

Least squares and residual updates

For $f(\beta)=\|y-X\beta\|^2/(2n)$, maintain $r=y-X\beta$. Then

\[\nabla_j f(\beta)=-X_{:,j}^\top r/n,\qquad a_j=\|X_{:,j}\|^2/n,\]

and for weighted NormL1,

\[\beta_j^+=S_{\lambda_0w_j/a_j} \left(\beta_j+\frac{X_{:,j}^\top r}{n a_j}\right),\qquad r^+=r-X_{:,j}(\beta_j^+-\beta_j).\]

For a nonzero column this exactly minimizes the quadratic objective along that coordinate. Each update costs O(n) for a dense column. The residual must be updated immediately so later coordinates see the current point.

using ProximalBase, LinearAlgebra
X = [1.0 0;0 2;1 1]
y = [2.0,-1,1]
n = length(y)
f = (1/n)*LeastSquaresLoss(y,X)
g = NormL1(0.2)
β = SparseIterate(2)
r = copy(y)
j = 1
a = sum(abs2,view(X,:,j))/n
old = β[j]
trial = old+dot(view(X,:,j),r)/(n*a)
β[j] = prox_block(g,trial,j,1/a)
r .-= view(X,:,j).*(β[j]-old)
@assert r ≈ y-X*β
@assert f(β)+g(β) <= f(zeros(2))
Vector(β)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.2
 0.0

Optimality and working sets

For convex differentiable $f$ and proper closed convex entry functions, $0\in\nabla_jf(x)+\partial g_j(x_j)$ for every j characterizes a minimizer. Here the subdifferential replaces the ordinary derivative at nonsmooth points. The weighted lasso conditions are

\[\begin{cases} \nabla_jf(x)+\lambda_0w_j\operatorname{sign}(x_j)=0,&x_j\ne0,\\ |\nabla_jf(x)|\le\lambda_0w_j,&x_j=0. \end{cases}\]

kkt_violation has three forms, with v the vector that the caller supplies, usually the gradient grad of the smooth part at x; it is not recomputed.

CallWhat it evaluatesSign
kkt_violation(g,v,j)The screen of coordinate j assumed zero: for NormL1, $\lvert v_j\rvert-\lambda_0w_j$; it reads no xSigned
kkt_violation(g,v,x,k)The score of the actual coordinate k of x (the group k for a BlockIterate)The signed margin at a zero coordinate; a nonnegative mismatch at a nonzero one
kkt_violation(g,v,x)$\max(0,\max_k s_k)$ over the entries of x, with $s_k$ the score of entry $k$Nonnegative

A positive score at a zero coordinate says that zero violates its condition, so the coordinate is a working-set candidate. A positive score at a nonzero coordinate is a stationarity mismatch; it is not an instruction to set the coordinate to zero. A negative score occurs only at a zero coordinate (or block) and is slack.

For an entry with $c_j=\lambda_0w_j$, NormL1 returns $\lvert v_j\rvert-c_j$ at a zero coordinate and $\lvert v_j+c_j\operatorname{sign}(x_j)\rvert$ at a nonzero one. ElasticNet returns the same at a zero coordinate with $c_j=\lambda_1w_j$, and $\lvert(v_j+\lambda_2x_j)+c_j\operatorname{sign}(x_j)\rvert$ at a nonzero one. For these two the full-point value is the $\ell_\infty$ distance from $-v$ to the subdifferential $\partial g(x)$ when the inputs are finite. For finite inputs, NormL1Nonneg returns $-v_j-c_j$ at a zero coordinate and $\lvert v_j+c_j\rvert$ at a positive one; the rules for infeasible and non-finite points are in the numerical contract. For ZeroFunction, LinearFunction and SqrNormL2 the score is $\lvert v_j+d_j\rvert$ at zero and nonzero coordinates alike, with $d_j$ the entry of $\nabla g(x)$ ($0$ for ZeroFunction, the coefficient for LinearFunction), and the full-point value is $\lVert v+\nabla g(x)\rVert_\infty$. The table of Mathematical operations lists each scalar.

using ProximalBase
g = NormL1(0.5)
x = [0.0, 0.0, 2.0, -1.0]
v = [0.8, 0.2, 0.0, 0.5]
screen = [kkt_violation(g, v, j) for j in 1:4]
scores = [kkt_violation(g, v, x, j) for j in 1:4]
(screen = screen, scores = scores, full_point = kkt_violation(g, v, x))
(screen = [0.30000000000000004, -0.3, -0.5, 0.0], scores = [0.30000000000000004, -0.3, 0.5, 0.0], full_point = 0.5)

At coordinate 3 the screen assumes $x_3=0$ and returns $-0.5$; the four-argument form reads $x_3=2$ and returns $0.5$. At coordinates 1 and 2 the two agree, $0.8-0.5>0$ (a candidate) and $0.2-0.5<0$ (slack), up to rounding, so compare them with ≈:

@assert screen[3] == -0.5 && scores[3] == 0.5 && scores[4] == 0
@assert scores[1] ≈ 0.3 && screen[1] ≈ 0.3
@assert scores[2] ≈ -0.3 && screen[2] ≈ -0.3
@assert kkt_violation(g, v, x) == 0.5

ProximalBase returns the raw score. Scaling, the tolerance, when to evaluate the score and the decision that a solver has converged belong to the caller. A solver that ranks inactive coordinates calls the three-argument or the four-argument form at coordinates of value zero; both give the signed screen there when x, v and the function's parameters (levels, weights, centers and coefficients) are Float32 or Float64 arrays and values of one type, v is finite and a SqrNormL2 level is not infinite. On a BlockIterate, the four-argument form takes the maximum of the entry scores of the group for the functions above, and GroupNormL2 scores the group (see The ℓ₂ and group ℓ₂ norms). GroupNormLinf gives the ℓ₁ screen ‖v_G‖₁ - λ0 w[k] of its dual norm at a zero group (#634), not the Euclidean screen of GroupNormL2, and the Euclidean distance from -v_G to the subdifferential at a nonzero group; the positive part of the screen is not a stationarity distance, and the full-point form scores a zero group by its distance to the ℓ₁ ball and not by its screen.

A working-set procedure optimizes selected coordinates, computes a full gradient to screen excluded zeros, adds violations, and repeats until both included and excluded coordinates satisfy their conditions. A restricted solve alone cannot certify the full problem. Stored coordinates include explicit zeros and need not equal the current mathematical support.

restrict(f,S) and restrict(g,S) slice supported function parameters, with local entry order given by S. For least squares and quadratics, they represent omitted variables fixed at zero. Centered SqrNormL2 slices its center and drops the omitted-coordinate constant $(λ/2)\sum_{j\notin S}y_j^2$. When that omitted contribution is finite, this changes objective values but not the restricted minimizers, gradients or prox; add the constant back when comparing full and restricted values. Nonzero fixed variables require a separately derived residual/linear term. Only vector-response LeastSquaresLoss has a restriction method. See Restricting functions for full mathematical definitions, nonseparable examples and the distinction between zero-fixed and nonzero-fixed subproblems, or the working-set example.

Block updates

For a partition $G_k$ and a block curvature $L_k>0$ majorizing changes in that block, a compatible decomposed penalty gives

\[x_{G_k}^+=\operatorname{prox}_{g_k/L_k} \left(x_{G_k}-\nabla_{G_k}f(x)/L_k\right).\]

Least squares can use $L_k=\|X_{:,G_k}\|_2^2/n$. This generally gives one upper-model step, rather than an exact block least-squares solution. Scalar column curvatures alone are not a block spectral bound.

BlockIterate supplies an ordered partition and public block views. A separable penalty uses scalar prox_block at each global member index. A custom block penalty supplies the compact-vector extension hook; the built-in GroupNormL2, GroupNormLinf and SparseGroupNormL2 implement it. See coordinate oracles, the block extension and the group-lasso building blocks. Matching layouts, constructing trials and updating caches are the caller's responsibilities.
