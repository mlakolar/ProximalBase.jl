Euclidean shrinkage and radial projection

Euclidean shrinkage moves a vector toward zero along its own direction. NormL2 applies it over the argument, and GroupNormL2 applies it to each group with its own threshold. IndBallL2 also uses a radial scale when projecting onto a Euclidean ball. See the NormL2 range rules, GroupNormL2 range rules, and IndBallL2 range rules.

Ordinary formulas

For a nonzero finite vector $x$, the closest point having norm $q$ lies in the direction of $x$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. The proximal problem for a nonnegative finite coefficient $c$ thus minimizes $\tfrac12(q-\lVert x\rVert_2)^2+cq$ over $q\ge0$. The resulting norm is $\max(\lVert x\rVert_2-c,0)$. With $n=\lVert x\rVert_2>c$, the exact proximal point is $(1-c/n)x$. When $n\le c$ it gives zero. For a group $G_k$, the same formula uses $x[G_k]$ and $c=\gamma\lambda_0w_k$.

For projection onto a ball of positive finite radius $r$, the scalar problem chooses $q=\min(\lVert x\rVert_2,r)$. The exact projection keeps a point on or inside the ball and gives $(r/n)x$ when $n>r$. Some computed paths check the rounded coefficients and rescale them toward zero, as described in the IndBallL2 range rules. For example, $x=(3,-4)$ has norm $5$. Shrinkage at $c=1$ gives $(12/5,-16/5)$, and projection at $r=5/2$ gives $(3/2,-2)$.

Range recovery

The shared shrinkage kernel uses the ordinary threshold and square sum when their range checks pass. For a finite nonzero group and positive finite factors on the recovery path, it represents the norm as $n_s2^k$. The scaled norm $n_s$ is the square root of the scaled sum of squares over the selected indices. The shrinkage decision then uses the ratio $(\gamma\lambda w\,2^{-k})/n_s$. The comparison and scaling can proceed when an unscaled intermediate is out of range.

The general ball projection route can compare $n_s$ with $r2^{-k}$ when it has a scaled norm. Its recovered write also carries the radius exponent separately. It divides the radius significand by $n_s$ before restoring the exponent in each output product. This keeps a tiny radius from rounding to zero during an intermediate division. Non-finite cases use the family endpoint rules for NormL2, GroupNormL2, and IndBallL2.
