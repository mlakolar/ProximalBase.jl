Scaled products

Proximal thresholds and weighted values combine factors such as a step, a penalty level, and a weight. For nonnegative coefficients, the shared product routines can keep a representable result from being lost when the first multiplication leaves the normal range. The shared range rules, NormL1 range rules, and Scaled range rules describe the behavior of the public calls.

Ordinary path

_positive_product keeps (a * b) * c when the first product is positive and normal. A range change in the final multiplication has no later factor to bring it back into range. For proximal thresholds, _threshold_product forms (γ * λ) * w in its working type, converts it to the requested threshold type, and reports whether γ * λ was positive and normal in the working type. A false flag sends the NormL1 threshold calculation to its exceptional path. The flag describes the first product, so a true flag can coexist with an infinite final threshold.

Exponent scaling

For nonzero finite factors, write each one as a significand times a power of two. The three-factor kernel multiplies the bounded significands and applies the sum of the exponents with ldexp. For instance, multiplying $2^{800}$ and $2^{400}$ first overflows in Float64, even though their product with $2^{-300}$ is $2^{900}$. Keeping the exponents separate also avoids starting the last multiplication with a rounded subnormal intermediate.

The weighted threshold path handles a stored infinite weight before using the finite-product recovery. NormL1 also checks its infinite-level rule in the exceptional branch. See the NormL1 endpoint rules.

When Scaled applies a factor to a value held as significand and exponent parts, the retained-exponent variant adds the exponents in Int. The stored exponent can already have wrapped. This addition recovers the intended combined exponent when it fits in Int.

Source and tests

  • Product helpers, exponent-scaled products, NormL1 threshold caller, and application of retained parts.
  • Bounded-product tests cover range changes and zero and non-finite factors in Float32 and Float64. Threshold tests exercise the flag and conversion separately. Retained-exponent tests exercise the wrapped exponent with BigFloat.
