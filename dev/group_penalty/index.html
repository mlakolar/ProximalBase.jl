The ℓ₂ and group ℓ₂ norms

Assumes: the ℓ₁ norm guide and grouped variables · You will learn: the Euclidean norm penalty, the group-lasso penalty, their proximal operators, the compact block form and the group optimality screen.

The ℓ₂ norm

NormL2(λ) is $g(x)=\lambda\lVert x\rVert_2$ (the Frobenius norm for a matrix) with $\lambda\ge0$. Its proximal operator shrinks the whole argument, $\max(1-\gamma\lambda/\lVert x\rVert_2,0)\,x$. On a nonempty argument it is the group ℓ₂ norm below with one group 1:length(x) of weight one, and follows the same rules, the exceptional cases included.

using ProximalBase, LinearAlgebra
h = NormL2(0.5)
@assert h([3.0, 4.0]) == 2.5
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 2.0) ≈ (1 - 1 / 5) * [3.0, 4.0]
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 10.0) == [0.0, 0.0]          # ‖x‖ = 5 ≤ γλ
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 1.0) == prox(GroupNormL2(0.5, [1:2], [1.0]), [3.0, 4.0], 1.0)
prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 2.0)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.4000000000000004
 3.2

The value takes arrays and the three iterates. On a SymmetricSparseIterate it is the Frobenius norm of the symmetric matrix: each stored off-diagonal coordinate counts twice in the squared norm. On a SparseIterate or a SymmetricSparseIterate it reads only the stored entries, also when a sum of squares that overflows or underflows is recomputed (#808). The proximal operators take arrays of the same size; a BlockIterate or a SparseIterate is read and written by indexing its entries, O(length) in general, as an array. A SymmetricSparseIterate has no NormL2 proximal operator: those calls throw an ArgumentError before anything is written. A zero level gives value zero. An infinite level gives zero at the zero argument (including an empty array), NaN at a NaN entry, and Inf otherwise. With unit group weight, the endpoint table also describes the norm's prox; in particular zero level takes precedence over an infinite step.

The NormLinf norm

NormLinf(λ) is $g(x)=\lambda\max_i\lvert x_i\rvert$ with $\lambda\ge0$; it is not separable. Its proximal operator clips the entries at a level $\tau\ge0$, $\operatorname{clamp}(x_i,-\tau,\tau)$, where $\sum_i(\lvert x_i\rvert-\tau)_+=\gamma\lambda$, and it is zero when $\gamma\lambda\ge\lVert x\rVert_1$. The level comes from the projection onto the ℓ₁ ball of radius $\gamma\lambda$ (Condat's algorithm, with Michelot's as a fallback), and the output is the clip itself, not $x$ minus that projection. The error of $\tau$ is absolute: on the ordinary finite path, at most $2.25\,n\,\varepsilon\max_i\lvert x_i\rvert+2n\delta$ for $n\ge1$ entries with $n\varepsilon\le 1/2$, relative to the radius $a$ that the proximal operator forms from $\gamma$ and $\lambda$ and the entries read in the output type, with $\varepsilon$ the machine epsilon and $\delta$ the smallest subnormal number of the output type (the storage of a subnormal radius or output adds $\delta$). The numerical contract states the hypotheses and the scaled units of the exceptional path. The value takes arrays and the three iterates, and a SymmetricSparseIterate (its largest stored magnitude); the proximal operators take arrays and the sparse and block iterates, and a SymmetricSparseIterate argument or output throws an ArgumentError before anything is written. The endpoint rules (a zero or infinite level or step, NaN and infinite entries, overflow of $\gamma\lambda$) are in the numerical contract. A NormLinf object may keep a buffer of its own, so that it must not be used from several tasks at once: construct a fresh NormLinf(f.λ) for each task, or synchronize the calls (ownership); the full-point kkt_violation below does not use that buffer.

hinf = NormLinf(1.0)
@assert hinf([3.0, -1.0]) == 3.0
@assert prox(hinf, [3.0, -1.0], 1.0) == [2.0, -1.0]          # τ = 2: Σ(|xᵢ| - τ)₊ = 1
@assert prox(hinf, [2.0, 1.0], 2.0) == [0.5, 0.5]            # τ = 0.5
@assert prox(hinf, [3.0, -1.0], 4.0) == [0.0, 0.0]           # ‖x‖₁ = 4 ≤ γλ
@assert prox(NormLinf(1.0), [5.0], 2.0) == prox(NormL1(1.0), [5.0], 2.0)   # one entry: soft thresholding
prox(hinf, [3.0, -1.0], 1.0)

Optimality score

For a smooth part h with gradient v = ∇h(x), the point x is stationary for h + g, with g = NormLinf(λ), when $-v\in\partial g(x)$. kkt_violation(g, v, x) is the Euclidean distance from $-v$ to $\partial g(x)$. It is nonnegative, and it is zero when $-v$ is a subgradient (in floating point, zero to rounding). With $J=\{i:\lvert x_i\rvert=\max_j\lvert x_j\rvert\}$, the subdifferential is the set of $s$ with $\lVert s\rVert_1\le\lambda$ at $x=0$. At $x\ne0$ it is the set of $s$ that vanish off $J$, satisfy $s_i\operatorname{sign}(x_i)\ge0$ on $J$ and have $\sum_{i\in J}\lvert s_i\rvert=\lambda$. With $u_i=-\operatorname{sign}(x_i)\,v_i$, the value is the following.

PointValueLevel $\tau$
$x=0$$0$ when $\sum_i\lvert v_i\rvert\le\lambda$; otherwise $\bigl(\sum_i\min(\lvert v_i\rvert,\tau)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$, the norm of prox(g, v, 1)$\tau\ge0$ with $\sum_i(\lvert v_i\rvert-\tau)_+=\lambda$
$x\ne0$$\bigl(\sum_{i\notin J}v_i^2+\sum_{i\in J}\min(u_i,\tau)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$$\tau$ with $\sum_{i\in J}(u_i-\tau)_+=\lambda$; it can be negative

The face $J$ is the set of entries whose magnitude equals the largest one after conversion to the working type of the call, compared for equality, with no tolerance. An entry one unit in the last place below the largest is not on the face, so for a positive finite level an exact tie can make the value discontinuous in x (for finite entries of v and x, the value is $\lVert v\rVert_2$ when $\lambda=0$ and Inf at a nonzero x when the level is infinite, ties or not). Where the clip of prox! is active (a positive finite level and step with $\gamma\lambda<\lVert x\rVert_1$), it writes the same level $\tau$, with the sign of the entry, into each clipped entry. The clipped entries of such a point have the same magnitude and belong to the maximal face, and entries already at that magnitude belong to it as well (prox(NormLinf(1.0), [3.0, 2.0, 1.0], 1.0) is [2.0, 2.0, 1.0]): this is the case the equality rule is made for. For an exact proximal point y = prox(g, z, γ) and v = (y - z)/γ, the vector $-v$ belongs to the subdifferential at y. With the computed proximal point and difference, the score is checked against a rounding tolerance, as in the example, which scores such a point and then the same v at a point where one clipped entry is one unit in the last place below the other:

fl = NormLinf(1.0)
# the zero point: inside the ℓ1 ball of radius 1 (value 0), and outside it
@assert kkt_violation(fl, [0.3, -0.5], [0.0, 0.0]) == 0.0
@assert kkt_violation(fl, [3.0, 1.0], [0.0, 0.0]) ≈ sqrt(5.0)               # τ = 2: residual (2, 1)
@assert kkt_violation(fl, [3.0, 1.0], [0.0, 0.0]) ≈ norm(prox(fl, [3.0, 1.0], 1.0))
# a face of one entry: |v₁ + λ| there (τ = -0.6), and v₂ off it
@assert kkt_violation(fl, [-0.4, 0.3], [2.0, 1.0]) ≈ sqrt(0.6^2 + 0.3^2)
# a point computed by prox: the clipped entries tie in magnitude, and the score is zero to rounding
@assert prox(fl, [3.0, 2.0, 1.0], 1.0) == [2.0, 2.0, 1.0]                  # an entry already at the magnitude belongs to the face
z = [3.0, -2.5, 0.5]
y = prox(fl, z, 1.0)
v = y - z
@assert count(==(maximum(abs, y)), abs.(y)) == 2
@assert kkt_violation(fl, v, y) <= 1e-14
# the same v with one clipped entry one unit in the last place below the other: the face is entry 1
y1 = copy(y); y1[2] = -prevfloat(abs(y[2]))
@assert kkt_violation(fl, v, y1) ≈ sqrt(0.125) rtol = 1e-6                  # τ = u₁ - 1 = -0.25, and v₂ = 0.25 off the face
(stationary = kkt_violation(fl, v, y), one_ulp_below = kkt_violation(fl, v, y1))
(stationary = 0.0, one_ulp_below = 0.3535533905932738)

For finite entries, a zero level gives $\lVert v\rVert_2$, and an infinite level gives 0 at the zero point and Inf at another point. A NaN entry of v or x gives NaN, also beside an infinite entry; otherwise an infinite entry gives Inf, also at a zero or an infinite level. The working type is float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))). The rules for the levels, the non-finite entries, the ranges and the accuracy are in the numerical contract.

The norm is not separable, so it has the full-point form and no form for an entry or a group. The calls kkt_violation(g, v, i) and kkt_violation(g, v, x, k) at an array, a SparseIterate, a BlockIterate or a SymmetricSparseIterate, throw an ArgumentError that names the full-point form. The full-point form at a SymmetricSparseIterate throws an ArgumentError. A SparseIterate or a BlockIterate in the full-point form is read as one vector, by indexing. A length n for which n + 1 is not a finite number of the working type (Float16 from n = 65519) throws an ArgumentError. For Array arguments of one type, Float32 or Float64, with a level of a hardware float type, the tests measure no allocation and no write to v, x or the buffer of the NormLinf object; wider parameter arithmetic such as a BigFloat level can allocate. Scaled(NormLinf(λ), α) with a finite positive α scores v/α and multiplies the result by α; a chain of wrappers whose factors fold into a normal level is scored with that level (the scaling guide has the limits and the refusals). test/kkt_linf.jl checks the value against BigFloat references written from the definition.

The group ℓ₂ norm

GroupNormL2 represents the group-lasso penalty

\[g(x)=\lambda_0\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2,\]

over disjoint groups $G_k$ of linear indices of x. The groups may have any sizes and orders, such as 1:5 or [7, 2, 9]; indices in no group are not penalized. The level $\lambda_0$ and the weights $w_k$ are nonnegative, and the weights default to $w_k=\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}$.

Mathematical formJulia constructorMeaning
$\lambda_0\sum_k\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}\,\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$GroupNormL2(λ0, groups)Default weights
$\lambda_0\sum_k w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$GroupNormL2(λ0, groups, w)One nonnegative weight per group
$G_k=\{k\}$ for every $k$GroupNormL2(λ0, [[i] for i in 1:p], w)The weighted ℓ₁ norm NormL1(λ0, w)

The constructor keeps its own copies of groups and w. A negative or NaN level or weight, an empty group, a nonpositive index and an index in two groups (or twice in one) throw an ArgumentError; a weight count that differs from the group count throws a DimensionMismatch. Groups retain their index order. Concrete range collections keep ranges; otherwise groups are copied into integer vectors. The level and weights are converted to their promoted floating-point type. These copies protect the function from later edits to constructor inputs; they do not make arbitrary changes to the function's fields valid. At an oracle call, every group index must fit the argument, and arrays must be real and one-based.

groups = [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3]]          # entry 6 is in no group
g = GroupNormL2(0.4, groups, [1.0, 3.0])
x = [1.0, -2.0, 0.5, 2.0, 0.0, 7.0]
@assert g(x) ≈ 0.4 * (norm(x[[1, 4, 2]]) + 3 * norm(x[[5, 3]]))
g(x)
1.8000000000000003

Proximal operator: group soft thresholding

For a step $\gamma\ge0$, the proximal operator acts on each group separately:

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}(x)_{G_k} =\max\left(1-\frac{\gamma\lambda_0w_k}{\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2},0\right)x_{G_k},\]

and leaves the entries in no group unchanged. A group whose norm is at most the threshold $\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ becomes zero; any other group keeps its direction and loses $\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ of its norm.

γ = 0.5
p = prox(g, x, γ)
@assert p[[1, 4, 2]] ≈ (1 - γ * 0.4 / 3) * x[[1, 4, 2]]   # ‖x[G₁]‖ = 3
@assert p[[5, 3]] == [0, 0]                                 # ‖x[G₂]‖ = 0.5 ≤ γ⋅0.4⋅3
@assert p[6] == x[6]
p
6-element Vector{Float64}:

 -1.8666666666666667
  0.0
  1.8666666666666667
  0.0
  7.0

prox!(y, g, x, γ) writes into y and accepts y === x. value_and_prox! also returns the value at the stored output. The step is a nonnegative scalar: a negative or NaN step throws an ArgumentError before anything is written.

Compact block operations

prox_block!(out, g, u, k, γ) computes the result for one group from a compact trial vector u, whose entries follow the order of groups[k], and writes it into out. It returns out, and out === u is supported. The group index, the buffer lengths, the step and the overlap of out and u are checked before any write. The buffers must be real, one-based vectors of the group's length. Any nonidentical overlap reported by Base.mightalias(out, u) throws ArgumentError. prox_block(g, u, k, γ) allocates the output.

The call does not write to or activate any iterate: u may be a view that reads one, and out must be independent of iterate storage, the caller precondition of the block contract. With a BlockIterate that has the groups of g (for example BlockIterate(p, g)), the caller builds the trial, computes the result in its own buffer and then writes it, which is the step that activates the group. BlockIterate(p, g) and BlockIterate(x, g) require the groups to partition every entry of the iterate, including entries unpenalized by a zero weight:

f = GroupNormL2(0.4, [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3], [6]])
z = BlockIterate(6, f)
u = [3.0, 4.0, 12.0]                        # ‖u‖ = 13
out = prox_block(f, u, 1, 1.0)
@assert out ≈ (1 - 0.4 * sqrt(3) / 13) * u
@assert nactive(z) == 0                     # the pure oracle changed nothing
copyto!(blockview(z, 1), out)
@assert collect(activecoordinates(z)) == [1]
out
3-element Vector{Float64}:
  2.840118386993642
  3.7868245159915226

The block storage guide describes the activation rule of these writes, and the pure block-prox contract the general extension hook.

Optimality scores

For a smooth part h with gradient G = ∇h(x), group $k$ is optimal for h + g at x when $0\in G_{G_k}+\partial g_k(x_{G_k})$. With $c_k=\lambda_0w_k$, kkt_violation(g, G, x, k) returns the score of the group:

Group of xScoreSign
zero$\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_2-c_k$, the screen of the groupsigned; a positive value means that zero violates the condition
not zero$\lVert G_{G_k}+c_k\,x_{G_k}/\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2\rVert_2$nonnegative; a stationarity mismatch, not an instruction to set the group to zero

A group is zero when its entries, converted to the working type of the call, are zeros of either sign. On an array point k is an entry and selects its group; the score is the same for each entry of the group. An entry in no group has the score $\lvert G_k\rvert$, also when x[k] is nonzero. On a BlockIterate, k is the group number and group k of the iterate must equal groups[k]. The three-argument form throws an ArgumentError, because the penalty is not separable, and a SymmetricSparseIterate has no GroupNormL2 score: the call throws an ArgumentError.

kkt_violation(g, G, x) is the full-point measure: the maximum of 0, of the positive scores of the groups and of $\lvert G_i\rvert$ over the entries in no group (including the entries past the largest index of a group). The positive parts of the group scores are Euclidean subgradient distances; the full-point measure takes their maximum together with the uncovered-entry magnitudes. It is not an $\ell_\infty$ distance, and a tolerance chosen for an $\ell_\infty$ residual does not carry over. It is zero of the working type for an empty x. It validates one-based axes, real entries, length(G) == length(x) and the group extent once, and on a BlockIterate the whole layout (its group count and each group), which is stricter than the local form, which compares group k. A NaN in an entry that a group reads gives NaN, and takes precedence over an infinite entry in another group.

In a score, a zero weight gives the coefficient 0 (also with an infinite level), and an infinite weight, or an infinite level with a positive weight, gives -Inf at a zero group and +Inf at a nonzero one (for finite entries). The working type W is float(promote_type(eltype(G), eltype(x))); the rules for non-finite entries and the accuracy are in the numerical contract.

G = [0.3, -0.4, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 2.0]
z0 = BlockIterate(6, f)                     # every group is zero
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z0, 1) ≈ 0.5 - 0.4 * sqrt(3)
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z0, 3) ≈ 2.0 - 0.4
z1 = BlockIterate(6, f)
copyto!(blockview(z1, 1), [1.0, 1.0, 1.0])  # group 1 is nonzero
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z1, 3) ≈ 2.0 - 0.4
# group 1 holds entries 1, 4, 2: G + 0.4 √3 x/‖x‖ = (0.7, 0.4, 0)
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z1, 1) ≈ sqrt(0.7^2 + 0.4^2)
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z1) ≈ 2.0 - 0.4     # the largest of the group scores
kkt_violation(f, G, z1, 1)
0.8062257748298549

The common interpretation of infinite penalties at zero step applies. A finite zero group becomes +0 under positive finite parameters. An infinite-weight or infinite-level group's value is zero at a zero group, NaN when it contains NaN, and Inf otherwise; a zero weight overrides these value rules and contributes zero. For finite inputs, norms whose sums of squares overflow or underflow and thresholds outside the floating-point range are recomputed in power-of-two units, so that a representable proximal point is returned accurately. Values recover terms that round below floatmin: for Float32 and Float64, a sum below K*floatmin, where K is the total number of groups, is recomputed using term exponents. Values at or above floatmax/2 are recomputed in power-of-two units and accumulated in at least Float64. Within a few eps of the overflow threshold the result may be floatmax or Inf, regardless of which one the exact value rounds to. See value range limits.

GroupNormL2 does not apply to a SymmetricSparseIterate (its proximal operators throw an ArgumentError before anything is written), and it has no restrict, no per-group step and no compact block method under Scaled. With a positive finite α, Scaled(GroupNormL2(...), α) multiplies the value by α, computes the array proximal operator with the step αγ, and scores a group by evaluating the score of GroupNormL2 on v/α and multiplying it by α (see the scaling guide for the limits and the unsupported cases); test/kkt_block.jl checks these scores against the definition of the scaled function. A zero α throws an ArgumentError, and an infinite α has no endpoint rule of its own. The group-lasso example checks the value, the proximal operators, the block operations and the screen against their formulas.

The group ℓ∞ norm

GroupNormLinf represents the group ℓ₁/ℓ∞ penalty

\[g(x)=\lambda_0\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_k\max_{i\in G_k}\lvert x_i\rvert,\]

over disjoint groups $G_k$ of linear indices of x. Its constructors follow those of GroupNormL2: groups of any sizes and orders, entries in no group unpenalized, the constructor's own copies of groups and w, a DimensionMismatch for a weight count that differs from the group count, and an ArgumentError for a converted level or weight that is negative or NaN, an empty group, a nonpositive index and an index in two groups. The weights default to $w_k=1$.

Proximal operator: group clipping

For a step $\gamma\ge0$ the proximal operator acts on each group separately. With the radius $r_k=\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ it clips the group at a level $\tau_k\ge0$,

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}(x)_i=\operatorname{clamp}(x_i,-\tau_k,\tau_k),\qquad \sum_{i\in G_k}\left(\lvert x_i\rvert-\tau_k\right)_+=r_k,\qquad i\in G_k,\]

and, for finite entries, the group becomes zero (+0) when the computed radius reaches the computed sum of magnitudes (see below; the table of exceptional parameters and values below gives the other cases). In exact arithmetic this is $x_{G_k}$ minus its projection onto the ℓ₁ ball of radius $r_k$, formed by the clip itself. The level comes from the same threshold as NormLinf, applied to the entries of one group, read through its indices without a copy. With finite data and positive finite parameters of the same type Float32 or Float64, and input and output of that type, the absolute error in each stored entry is at most $8n\varepsilon\max_{i\in G_k}\lvert x_i\rvert+(2n+1)\delta$ under the assumptions in the numerical contract. Here $n$ is the group size, $\varepsilon$ is eps(T) and $\delta$ is the smallest positive subnormal of T. The radius is formed as $(\gamma\lambda_0)w_k$ in floating point and then converted to the output type. When $\gamma\lambda_0$ is not a normal number, or the product leaves the normal range of the output type, it is formed from the significands and exponents of the three factors, so that an intermediate overflow or underflow does not decide it. The accuracy bound includes the case where the computed radius reaches the computed sum and the entire group becomes +0.

gi = GroupNormLinf(1.0, [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3]], [1.0, 2.0])      # entry 6 is in no group
xi = [3.0, -1.0, 0.5, 2.0, 4.0, 7.0]
@assert gi(xi) == 1.0 * 3.0 + 2.0 * 4.0
pg = prox(gi, xi, 0.5)
@assert pg == [2.5, -1.0, 0.5, 2.0, 3.0, 7.0]       # radii 0.5 (τ = 2.5) and 1 (τ = 3); entry 6 is unchanged

prox!(y, g, x, γ) writes into y and accepts y === x; value_and_prox! also returns the value at the stored output, which it forms from the clip levels (for an output whose element type is not floating point, the value is recomputed from the stored output, after the conversions of its entries succeed). The step is a nonnegative scalar: a negative or NaN step throws an ArgumentError before anything is written. A BlockIterate or a SparseIterate is read and written by indexing its entries, as for GroupNormL2; a SymmetricSparseIterate has no GroupNormLinf proximal operator (an ArgumentError before any write).

prox_block!(out, g, u, k, γ) computes the result for one group from a compact trial vector u in the order of groups[k], with the checks, the aliasing rules and the commit through copyto!(blockview(x, k), out) of the group ℓ₂ norm. BlockIterate(p, g) and BlockIterate(x, g) copy the groups of g and require them to partition the iterate.

gp = GroupNormLinf(1.0, [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3], [6]], [1.0, 2.0, 1.0])

A GroupNormLinf object keeps a buffer for the threshold, with the length of its longest group, which the first group that needs one allocates. The buffer is left untouched by the parameter exits, by NaN and infinite groups, by a zero fill decided before the threshold (the computed radius reaches the computed sum of magnitudes; a threshold whose level is zero has already used the buffer) and, in prox_block!, when out is an Array with a floating-point element type, u is an Array or a SubArray of an Array, and !Base.mightalias(out, u) (so not when out === u): out is then the buffer. The same object must not be used from several tasks at once while its buffer may be in use (Scaled wrappers of one object share it, and so do the groups of a threaded loop of prox_block!(u, g, u, k, γ)), and it must not be used reentrantly, also within one task: a nested call on the same object, from a getindex of an argument while the threshold runs, can overwrite the candidates of the running threshold if it reaches the threshold with the shared buffer. Construct a fresh GroupNormLinf for each task, or synchronize the calls (ownership).

Optimality score at a zero group

The dual norm of the ℓ∞ norm is the ℓ₁ norm. At a zero group the subdifferential of the penalty is the ℓ₁ ball of radius $c_k=\lambda_0w_k$, so $\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_1\le c_k$, with G = ∇h(x), is the first-order (stationarity) condition of the zero group for h + g. It certifies that the block is optimal for h + g when h is convex in that block. kkt_violation(g, G, x, k) returns the signed margin of that condition:

Group of xScoreSign
zero$\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_1-c_k$signed; a positive value means that zero violates the condition
not zeroArgumentErrorthe score of a nonzero group is not provided

The group is selected as for GroupNormL2: on an array point k is an entry, which selects its group (an entry in no group has the score $\lvert G_k\rvert$), and on a BlockIterate it is the group number, whose layout must equal that of g. The three-argument form kkt_violation(g, G, k) throws an ArgumentError because the penalty is not separable, as does the full-point form kkt_violation(g, G, x), which is not provided for this function, and a SymmetricSparseIterate has no groups. Under Scaled, the four-argument and full-point forms have no method (a MethodError). The margin is the screen of the dual norm, which is not the Euclidean screen of GroupNormL2 (#634): the two differ in sign for the gradient (5/8, 5/8) with $\lambda_0=w=1$, where the group must enter. The positive part of the margin is an ℓ₁ screen and not a stationarity distance, so a tolerance chosen for another score does not carry over. In the margin, a NaN in $G_{G_k}$ gives NaN, and an infinite entry (with no NaN) or a computed sum of magnitudes that overflows gives +Inf, also when $c_k$ is infinite (that branch comes first); an infinite $c_k$ gives -Inf when the computed sum of magnitudes is finite, also where $\lambda_0w_k$ overflows in the working type; a zero weight gives $\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_1$. A group is zero when its entries, converted to the working type of the call (the floating type of promote_type(eltype(G), eltype(x))), are zeros of either sign, so a NaN or infinite entry of x makes its group nonzero.

v = [0.3, -0.4, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0, 2.0]
zb = BlockIterate(6, gp)                       # every group is zero
@assert kkt_violation(gp, v, zb, 1) ≈ 0.2      # ‖(0.3, 0.5, -0.4)‖₁ - 1
@assert kkt_violation(gp, v, zb, 2) == -1.0    # ‖(0.0, 1.0)‖₁ - 2
@assert kkt_violation(gp, v, zb, 3) == 1.0     # ‖(2.0,)‖₁ - 1
@assert kkt_violation(GroupNormLinf(1.0, [1:2]), [5/8, 5/8], zeros(2), 1) == 0.25
@assert kkt_violation(GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2], [1.0]), [5/8, 5/8], zeros(2), 1) < 0
copyto!(blockview(zb, 1), [3.0, 2.0, -1.0])    # group 1 is nonzero
nonzero_group = try kkt_violation(gp, v, zb, 1); false catch e; e isa ArgumentError end
@assert nonzero_group && kkt_violation(gp, v, zb, 2) == -1.0

Exceptional parameters and values

The rules apply to each group in this order; $\lambda_0$, $w_k$ and $\gamma$ are decided separately, before a radius is formed, so that the proximal operator and the value do not form a product such as $\lambda_0w_k$ with $\lambda_0=\infty$ and $w_k=0$.

$w_k=\infty$, or $\lambda_0=\infty$ with $w_k>0$Fixed at +0 at every nonnegative step, $\gamma=0$ and $\gamma=\infty$ included, for NaN and ±Inf inputs too; $w_k=\infty$ with $\lambda_0=0$ is included. Value 0 at a zero group, Inf at a nonzero group without NaN, NaN at a NaN entry

A NaN entry, positive finite level, weight and stepEvery entry of the group becomes NaN; the other groups are unaffected

The value of a group is $\lambda_0w_k\max\lvert x_{G_k}\rvert$, formed in floating point as $(\lambda_0w_k)m$ with two rounded products when $\lambda_0w_k$ is a normal number and by the three-factor kernel in power-of-two units otherwise, and then converted to the output type; the sum over the groups may be recomputed in power-of-two units, accumulated in at least Float64, where it may have lost accuracy (a sum below K*floatmin(T) for K groups, whose terms may have rounded into the subnormal range) or class (at or above floatmax(T)/2), as for GroupNormL2 (value range limits). A sum of magnitudes of one group above floatmax(T)/2 is processed in power-of-two units. With a positive finite level and weight, prox! computes the clip of a group with finite entries whose sum overflows from the entries scaled by a power of two:

M = floatmax(Float64)
huge = GroupNormLinf(M, [1:2], [2.0])        # the radius γλ₀w = M/2 at γ = 0.25 is finite; λ₀w alone overflows
yh = prox(huge, [M, M], 0.25)
@assert yh[1] == yh[2] && isapprox(yh[1], 0.75M; rtol = 1e-14)
tiny = GroupNormLinf(2.0^-100, [1:2], [2.0^1000])   # γλ₀ underflows, γλ₀w = 2^-100 equals ‖x‖₁
@assert prox(tiny, [2.0^-100, 0.0], 2.0^-1000) == [0.0, 0.0]

GroupNormLinf has no SymmetricSparseIterate, restrict, per-group step or compact block method under Scaled. Scaled(GroupNormLinf(...), α) multiplies the value by α and computes the array proximal operator with the step αγ; it has no folded level, so a step αγ that overflows reaches the function as Inf. A group that reaches the infinite-step exit then gives +0, where the exact radius $\alpha\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ can be below the ℓ₁ norm of the group; a group with a zero weight or a zero level with a finite weight keeps the identity, because those exits come first. A zero α throws an ArgumentError.

scaled = Scaled(GroupNormLinf(2.0^-1020, [1:2]), 2.0^1000)
@assert prox(scaled, [2000.0, 500.0], 2.0^30) == [0.0, 0.0]           # the step 2^1030 overflows
@assert prox(GroupNormLinf(2.0^-20, [1:2]), [2000.0, 500.0], 2.0^30) == [976.0, 500.0]   # the radius 2^10 = 1024 keeps the group
