Coordinate and block operations

Assumes: scalar proximal updates and coordinate indexing · You will learn: how to compute a coordinate result separately from writing it to storage.

These are proximal and coordinate operations; they do not select a solver's working set.

The pure mathematical hook computes a proximal point at a supplied trial. The solver then writes the result to storage. ProximalBase does not offer an operation that both computes a coordinate proximal point and changes the iterate's active set.

An active slot is never a coordinate or group index. BlockIterate has a storage partition, not a penalty: NormL1 has no groups and uses each global member index. ProximalBase.samegroups accepts two BlockIterates; custom functions must provide their own layout comparison or compare lists with blockindices.

using ProximalBase
x = SparseIterate(4)
x[1] = 0.5
trial = x[1]  # an algorithm would include its gradient step here
new = prox_block(NormL1(1.0),trial,1,1.0)
@assert x[1] == 0.5
x[1] = new
@assert x[1] == 0 && isactive(x,1)
x[2] = prox_block(NormL1(1.0),3.0,2,1.0)
@assert x[2] == 2 && isactive(x,2)
Vector(x)

Compact block buffers

The generic vector prox_block allocates a plain floating Vector of length(u) and invokes prox_block!. GroupNormL2, GroupNormLinf, SparseGroupNormL2 and GroupMCP are the built-in group implementations. k selects f.groups[k], and u follows that group's ordered global indices. These methods check the group, buffer lengths, real one-based storage, step and overlap before writes. Custom extensions must provide these checks themselves. Exact out === u is supported by the contract; other overlap must be rejected by the extension. The generic allocating wrapper adds no such checks on the extension's behalf.

Caller precondition: output must be independent of iterate storage and solver/model caches. A view such as activevalues(x), coordinates(x) or blockview(x,k) aliases storage even when its parent is a plain vector. The mathematical hook cannot establish ownership from array types. Compute in an independent numerical vector, then use the public storage writer. An exception inside a custom numerical computation can leave partial numerical output; keeping it independent protects the iterate until the separate write.

The block extension is a complete checked implementation. Implementing this hook does not automatically supply a full array value, full-array prox, traits, kkt_violation method, symmetric-iterate interface or Scaled compact-vector forwarding.

Scoring a coordinate

kkt_violation(g,v,i) reports the signed screen of coordinate i assumed zero. NormL1 returns $|v_i|-\lambda_0w_i$. A positive value means zero fails the condition. The routine does not inspect whether the coordinate is actually zero, and it takes no iterate.

kkt_violation(g,v,x,k) reads x and interprets k in x's coordinate space. For NormL1, ElasticNet, ZeroFunction, LinearFunction and SqrNormL2 it returns the signed screen at a zero coordinate and a nonnegative stationarity mismatch at a nonzero one (see Mathematical operations for each scalar). For NormL1Nonneg its point-aware entry formulas and non-finite overrides are described in Mathematical operations and the numerical contract. GroupNormL2 has the same two scores for a group, and Scaled forwards them (below). GroupNormLinf has the signed margin $\lVert v_G\rVert_1-c$ of its dual norm at a zero group, where c = λ0 w[k], and, at a nonzero group, the Euclidean distance from $-v_G$ to the subdifferential of $c\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ at $x_G$; its margin is the ℓ₁ screen of #634, and its positive part is not a stationarity distance. kkt_violation(g,v,x) is the nonnegative full-point measure $\max(0,\max_k s_k)$ of the six scalar functions and GroupNormL2 on a one-based array, SparseIterate or BlockIterate: the scores $s_k$ of the entries for the six scalar functions, and for GroupNormL2 the scores of the groups and $\lvert v_i\rvert$ of the entries in no group, a maximum of block 2-norms and not an $\ell_\infty$ distance. For GroupNormLinf it is the maximum of zero, of $\lvert v_i\rvert$ of the entries in no group and of the distance of every group, a zero group included, which there is the distance from $v_G$ to the ℓ₁ ball of radius $c$ and not the margin of the four-argument form. NormL2 and NormLinf have the full-point form and no other.

SparseGroupNormL2 also provides the two point-aware forms. At a zero group it screens the soft-thresholded gradient. At an active group it measures the block subgradient distance. The full-point score takes the maximum with zero and includes the free-coordinate magnitudes.

GroupMCP has the four-argument form at a zero group, the screen of GroupNormL2 with the level and the weight as separate factors ($\lVert v_G\rVert_2-\lambda_0w_k$ for finite v, λ0 and w_k; the GroupMCP failure behavior gives the other cases), and abs(v[k]) at an entry in no group. At a real array point it throws an ArgumentError at a nonzero group and in the full-point form. A zero score does not imply a global minimizer.

At an array that is not a BlockIterate and at a SparseIterate, where k is an entry index, a call of the four-argument form for NormL1 and ElasticNet reads x[k], evaluates the score of a zero entry and that of a nonzero one, and selects between them; SqrNormL2 forms its gradient entry from x[k] (and from the center, when it has one); and each of the five functions applies the rules for non-finite values to the entries of v and x that its formula uses. A call therefore does more work than a call of the three-argument screen. For these five functions the caller chooses between the forms for a coordinate that it knows to be zero: at those points, the three-argument form returns the same value as the four-argument form with the same index when v, x and the parameters of the function (levels, weights, centers, coefficients) are Float32 or Float64 arrays and values of one type, v[k] is finite and the function is not a SqrNormL2 with an infinite level. A BlockIterate, where k is a group and the form takes the maximum over the entries of the group, and a SymmetricSparseIterate, where the form reads the symmetric gradient pair and the stored value, are not covered by this statement.

For SparseIterate/SymmetricSparseIterate, invalid coordinate throws BoundsError before extent mismatch throws DimensionMismatch, after checking one-based axes and real element types. A flat or matrix-shaped gradient of the required entry count is accepted. Opposing infinities in the selected symmetric gradient pair throw ArgumentError. Parameter extents are checked for symmetric iterates; mirrored parameters are a caller precondition, not an oracle check; the three-argument form takes no iterate and makes no parameter-symmetry check. GroupNormL2 checks the number of groups, the selected ordered group layout and length(v) == length(x), then scores the group Gₖ with $c=\lambda_0w_k$: norm(v[Gₖ]) - c at a zero group and norm(v[Gₖ] + c*x[Gₖ]/norm(x[Gₖ])) at a nonzero one (see the group penalty). Its three-argument form throws ArgumentError because the penalty is not entrywise separable, as does a SymmetricSparseIterate point. NormL2 takes the whole of x as one block and has the full-point form and no other: its three-argument and four-argument forms throw ArgumentError. NormLinf is not a block score either: its full-point form is the Euclidean distance from $-v$ to the subdifferential of $\lambda\max_i\lvert x_i\rvert$ at x (at $x=0$, the distance from v to the ℓ₁ ball of radius $\lambda$; at $x\ne0$, with the face of the entries of largest magnitude, compared for equality after conversion to the working type; see the ℓ∞ norm), and its three-argument and four-argument forms throw ArgumentError.

LeastSquaresLoss and QuadraticFunction use the full-point form to return ‖v + ∇f(x)‖∞. Inputs must have the same shape and must be independent of the scratch the function writes. See smooth stationarity scores for accepted layouts, rounding, allocations and scratch ownership.

Scaled with a finite positive factor α evaluates the score of its function on v/α and multiplies it by α, for the entrywise and group functions above (GroupNormLinf included) and, in the full-point form, NormL2, NormLinf, LeastSquaresLoss and QuadraticFunction (the rounding and range limits of that evaluation are in the scaling guide). Nested wrappers whose factors do not multiply to a normal number, an infinite factor, and the functions without a score have unsupported cases, which are listed in the scaling guide, and a function without a score gives a MethodError for the call on the Scaled function.

For the penalty, zero, linear and squared-norm functions above, the full-point form checks real element types, one-based axes (ArgumentError), length(v) == length(x) (DimensionMismatch) and the extent of the function's parameters (for GroupNormL2 the group extent and, on a BlockIterate, the whole layout: the group count and each group; for NormL2 and NormLinf no parameter). It makes a pass over the entries (over the groups for GroupNormL2) and writes nothing. A second pass that applies the rules for non-finite values entry by entry can follow it, for instance when an entry of v or x is not finite or a score is NaN or infinite. NormLinf makes several passes over v and x instead: one classification of each, the sums on the face, the search for the level, which makes one pass for each level that the iteration visits, and the sum of squares of the residual. The tests measure no allocation of this form for NormLinf on Float32 or Float64 arrays of one type with a level of a hardware float type; a BigFloat level can allocate. Dense Float32 or Float64 arrays of one type take the fast path of the other functions, where the tests measure no allocation; other arrays, SparseIterate and BlockIterate included, are read by entry, and element types such as BigFloat can allocate in their arithmetic. The four-argument form on an array reads the parameters of entry k. The element type, the rules for non-finite values and the rounding error are in the numerical contract.

A function defined outside the package gets its three-argument method's value from the four-argument form at a zero coordinate (a group of zero entries) and an ArgumentError at a nonzero one, unless it defines the four-argument method itself; under Scaled it has the scaled three-argument value and that error also when it defines the method; see Writing your own function. The built-in functions without a score, such as SqrtLeastSquaresLoss, throw MethodError. A positive score at a nonzero coordinate is a stationarity mismatch; see coordinate descent.

Symmetric scalar steps

For symmetric-iterate coordinate k, compute (r,c)=tril_cartesian(p,k), then use the full-matrix entry index i=(c-1)p+r. The function's parameters must be symmetric; validate the original function with check_symmetric_parameters before raw scalar hooks, since the hook has no symmetric iterate to inspect and no numerical oracle compares mirrored parameters. Use a symmetric matrix gradient and the appropriate Frobenius curvature.

using ProximalBase
x = SymmetricSparseIterate(3)
k = tril_index(3,3,2)
g = NormL1(0.5,ones(3,3))
ProximalBase.check_symmetric_parameters(g,x)
r,c = tril_cartesian(3,k)
i = (c-1)*3+r
coordinates(x)[k] = prox_block(g,2.0,i,1.0)
@assert x[3,2] == x[2,3] == 1.5
Matrix(x)
