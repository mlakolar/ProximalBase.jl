Coordinate oracles

prox_coordinate! and the four-argument kkt_violation act on one coordinate $k$ of an iterate: an entry of a SparseIterate, or a group of an AtomIterate (see Entries and coordinates). This section states what they do on these two iterates. For a SymmetricSparseIterate, see also What each call checks.

Steps on one coordinate

prox_coordinate!(x, f, k, γ) applies the proximal operator of $\gamma f$ to the current value of coordinate $k$. prox_coordinate!(x, f, k, u, γ) applies it to a trial value u instead, without writing u into x first. The trial is a number for a SparseIterate, and a vector of the group's length for an AtomIterate. Both forms write the result into coordinate $k$ and return it: the new value of the entry for a SparseIterate, and atom(x, k) for an AtomIterate.

Only coordinate $k$ can join the working set:

  • A coordinate that is absent and whose result is zero stays absent.
  • A coordinate whose result is nonzero becomes active.
  • An active coordinate stays active, even when the step sets it to zero. No coordinate is removed, and no other coordinate changes.

Both forms support separable functions on both iterates. On an AtomIterate they also support the group penalties of the package (GroupNormL2, GroupNormLinf, SparseGroupNormL2, GroupMCP and GroupSCAD) whose groups are those of x, and their Scaled and Regularize versions. The arguments are checked before anything is written, and after each of these errors x is unchanged:

ErrorCause
BoundsErrork is not a coordinate of x
DimensionMismatchthe groups of a group penalty differ from those of x (see The groups of an iterate)
ArgumentErrorany other function, such as a group penalty on a SparseIterate, IndBallGroupL0, or Precompose of a group penalty with an array
DimensionMismatcha parameter array, such as the weights of NormL1 or the center of Regularize, whose length is not length(x)
ArgumentErrora trial u whose element type is not real
DimensionMismatchfor an AtomIterate, a trial u whose length is not the size of group $k$

On an AtomIterate, an error outside this list, such as an entry of u whose reading throws or a value 1.5 for an iterate of integers, can leave entries of the group written. A group with a nonzero entry is then active.

using ProximalBase

x = SparseIterate(4)
x[1] = 0.5
v = prox_coordinate!(x, NormL1(1.0), 1, 1.0)        # soft thresholding of 0.5 at 1
@assert v == 0 && isactive(x, 1)                    # an active entry stays active at zero
v = prox_coordinate!(x, NormL1(1.0), 2, 3.0, 1.0)   # the trial value 3.0 is not written first
@assert v == 2.0 == x[2] && isactive(x, 2)
prox_coordinate!(x, NormL1(1.0), 3, 0.5, 1.0)       # a zero result
@assert !isactive(x, 3)                             # leaves an absent entry absent
try
    prox_coordinate!(x, NormL1(1.0, ones(3)), 4, 1.0)   # three weights for four entries
    error("expected a DimensionMismatch")
catch e
    @assert e isa DimensionMismatch
end
@assert Vector(x) == [0.0, 2.0, 0.0, 0.0]
Vector(x)

A trial u that shares memory with x, such as atom(x, j) or a view of x with its entries permuted, is copied before anything is written, so the result does not depend on the order of the writes. Here the trial is group 1 itself in reverse order. Writing its entries one at a time without the copy would give the trial $(4, 4)$ and the result $(3, 3)$.

f = GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2, 3:4])                    # weights √2
a = AtomIterate([3.0, 4.0, 0.0, 0.0], f)
r = prox_coordinate!(a, f, 1, view(a, [2, 1]), 1.0) # the trial (4, 3)
@assert collect(r) ≈ (1 - sqrt(2) / 5) .* [4.0, 3.0]
@assert r == atom(a, 1)

Block trials

Block coordinate descent computes a trial point $u$ for group $k$ (see AtomIterate). The supported way to take the group step at $u$ is prox_coordinate!(x, f, k, u, γ). It gives the values that writing u into group $k$ and then calling prox_coordinate!(x, f, k, γ) gives. The working sets differ: writing a nonzero u activates the group, while the five-argument form activates it only when the result is nonzero. A group whose step ends at zero therefore leaves no trace in the working set. After an error of the list in Steps on one coordinate, x is unchanged.

using ProximalBase

f = GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2, 3:4])
x = AtomIterate(4, f)
r = prox_coordinate!(x, f, 1, [3.0, 4.0], 1.0)
@assert collect(r) ≈ (1 - sqrt(2) / 5) .* [3.0, 4.0]
@assert collect(activecoordinates(x)) == [1]
prox_coordinate!(x, f, 2, [0.5, 0.5], 1.0)          # the norm of u is below √2
@assert iszero(atom(x, 2)) && !isactive(x, 2)

z = AtomIterate(4, f)                               # writing the trial first
z[3] = 0.5
z[4] = 0.5
prox_coordinate!(z, f, 2, 1.0)
@assert iszero(atom(z, 2)) && isactive(z, 2)        # same values, but the write activated group 2

before = (Vector(x), collect(activecoordinates(x)))
try
    prox_coordinate!(x, GroupNormL2(1.0, [[1, 3], [2, 4]]), 1, [9.0, 9.0], 1.0)
    error("expected a DimensionMismatch")
catch e
    @assert e isa DimensionMismatch
end
@assert (Vector(x), collect(activecoordinates(x))) == before

Do not bypass these methods by writing directly into the iterate's private storage. Use the five-argument prox_coordinate! form for block trial points; it keeps the working set consistent even when the trial is discarded.

Screening a zero coordinate

kkt_violation(f, g, x, k), with g the gradient of the smooth part at x, screens coordinate $k$ at zero. A positive value means that the coordinate, if it is zero, should enter the working set, and a value $\le 0$ that zero satisfies its optimality condition. It is not a stationarity residual of a nonzero coordinate.

On an AtomIterate, $k$ is a group. For a group penalty, the screen uses the penalty's own dual norm and weights, and it does not read the values of x in group $k$:

FunctionScreen of group $k$
GroupNormL2(λ0, groups, w)$\lVert g_{G_k}\rVert_2 - \lambda_0 w_k$
GroupNormLinf(λ0, groups, w)$\lVert g_{G_k}\rVert_1 - \lambda_0 w_k$
SparseGroupNormL2(λ1, λ2, groups, w)$\lVert S_{\lambda_1}(g_{G_k})\rVert_2 - \lambda_2 w_k$, with $S_{\lambda_1}$ soft thresholding at $\lambda_1$
GroupMCP(λ, groups, w), GroupSCAD(λ, groups, w)$\lVert g_{G_k}\rVert_2 - \lambda w_k$
Scaled(f, α) with $\alpha > 0$$\alpha$ times the screen of f at $g/\alpha$
Regularize(f, ρ, c)the screen of f at $g - \rho c$, or at $g$ without c
a separable fthe largest kkt_violation(f, g, i) over the entries $i$ of group $k$

The weights $w_k$ are those of the penalty, with the defaults of its docstring: the square root of the group size, and 1 for GroupNormLinf. Scaled(f, 0) is the function that Scaled builds at zero scale. For a group penalty with finite levels, a finite shape parameter (a of GroupMCP and GroupSCAD) and finite weights this is the zero function, whose screen on an AtomIterate is maximum(abs, view(g, atomindices(x, k))); a level or shape parameter that is not finite makes Scaled(f, 0) throw an ArgumentError when it is built. Otherwise, as with an infinite weight or an indicator, the screen is that of the function Scaled builds.

The table is not exhaustive. IndBallGroupL0 has no screen and throws a MethodError, and so do some functions without a screen of their own, such as NormL2. Other functions behave differently: the array Precompose of a group penalty has a screen on an AtomIterate (see Entrywise scaling), and MoreauEnvelope of a function that is not separable throws an ArgumentError. On an AtomIterate, the call throws a DimensionMismatch when the groups of a group penalty differ from those of x, and a BoundsError when $k$ is not a group.

The screens differ in sign where it matters. At $g = (5/8, 5/8)$ with $\lambda_0 = 1$, the screen of GroupNormLinf is 0.25, so the group must enter, while the ℓ₂ formula with weight 1 is about $-0.116$ and would screen the group out.

using ProximalBase

x2 = AtomIterate(2, [1:2])
g = [5 / 8, 5 / 8]
v = kkt_violation(GroupNormLinf(1.0, [1:2]), g, x2, 1)
@assert v == 0.25                                   # ‖g‖₁ − 1 > 0: the group must enter
@assert sqrt(sum(abs2, g)) - 1 ≈ -0.11611652351681556   # the ℓ₂ formula would screen it out
@assert kkt_violation(GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2]), g, x2, 1) ≈ sqrt(sum(abs2, g)) - sqrt(2)
@assert kkt_violation(Scaled(GroupNormLinf(1.0, [1:2]), 2.0), g, x2, 1) == 2 * (sum(g) / 2 - 1)
@assert kkt_violation(Regularize(GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2]), 1.0, [1.0, 1.0]), [3.0, 4.0], x2, 1) ≈
        sqrt(13) - sqrt(2)                          # the screen at (3, 4) − (1, 1)
v
0.25

On a SparseIterate, $k$ is an entry. After the check of the real element types, the call checks that $k$ is one of 1:ncoordinates(x) (a BoundsError otherwise) and then that g has the length of x (a DimensionMismatch otherwise); a matrix-shaped or flat g of that length is accepted; these two checks do not scan g. For a separable f the call then returns kkt_violation(f, g, k). A group penalty screens each entry with its group. When every entry of the group is zero in x, the call returns the screen of the group in the table. Otherwise it returns the screen of an entry at zero inside a nonzero group, abs(g[k]), or abs(g[k]) - λ1 for SparseGroupNormL2. An entry in no group gives abs(g[k]).

f = GroupNormL2(1.0, [1:2, 3:4])
y = SparseIterate(4)
gs = [3.0, 4.0, 0.0, 0.0]
@assert kkt_violation(f, gs, y, 1) ≈ 5 - sqrt(2)    # group 1 of y is zero: the group screen
y[2] = 1.0
@assert kkt_violation(f, gs, y, 1) == 3.0           # entry 1 of a nonzero group: abs(gs[1])
