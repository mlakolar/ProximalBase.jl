Sparse iterates

ProximalBase provides three iterate types for coordinate descent and active-set procedures. We separate the choice of storage from the update formulas, which are derived in Coordinate descent.

IterateArray representedOne coordinate
SparseIterateA sparse vector or matrixOne entry
SymmetricSparseIterateA sparse symmetric matrixOne lower-triangular entry and its symmetric counterpart
AtomIterateA vector partitioned into groupsOne group of entries

Entries and coordinates

prox_entry(g, v, i, γ) computes the scalar proximal operator of entry $i$ without writing the iterate. In contrast, prox_coordinate!(x, g, k, γ) reads coordinate $k$ of the iterate, applies its proximal operator and writes the result. The calling procedure must supply the gradient step before applying this proximal operation.

The two calls index different spaces:

  • prox_entry takes an entry $i$ of the array, the index of the weights and bounds.
  • prox_coordinate! takes a coordinate $k$ of the iterate.

The two coincide for a SparseIterate. A coordinate is an entry of the lower triangle for a SymmetricSparseIterate, and a group for an AtomIterate.

SparseIterate

A SparseIterate is a vector or matrix that stores its nonzero entries in a list. Three arrays hold them: the values, their indices, and for every index the position of its value in the list (zero when it is not stored). Reading or writing any entry is therefore $O(1)$, and a product $Xx$ costs $O(n\cdot\mathrm{nnz})$. The stored entries are the active coordinates (activecoordinates, nactive). Setting an entry to zero keeps it stored, so that an active set does not change during a sweep. dropzeros! removes the zeros between sweeps.

SymmetricSparseIterate

A SymmetricSparseIterate is a sparse symmetric $p \times p$ matrix that stores its lower triangle. For indexing, length, iteration and broadcasting it is an ordinary $p \times p$ matrix, except that writing $x_{rc}$ also writes $x_{cr}$. Its coordinates are the $p(p+1)/2$ entries $(r, c)$, $r \ge c$, of the lower triangle, numbered column by column:

\[k = \texttt{tril\_index}(p, r, c) = \frac{(c - 1)(2p - c + 2)}{2} + (r - c + 1).\]

tril_cartesian inverts this by solving a quadratic equation. Its floating square root is exact for $p < 2^{24}$, far beyond any matrix that fits in memory, and it switches to an integer square root beyond that. coordinates(x) is a vector view of the coordinates.

The coordinate step on a symmetric matrix. A coordinate $(r, c)$ with $r \ne c$ moves two entries of $\Theta$ together. Take a penalty that counts both triangles, $\lambda\sum_{i,j}|\Theta_{ij}|$, as NormL1 does on the matrix. Let $G$ be the gradient of $f$ over symmetric matrices, and $a$ the curvature along the direction $E_{rc} + E_{cr}$. Along the coordinate,

\[\varphi(t) = 2G_{rc}(t - t_0) + \frac{a}{2}(t - t_0)^2 + 2\lambda|t| + \text{const}, \qquad t^+ = S_{2\lambda/a}\Big(t_0 - \frac{2G_{rc}}{a}\Big).\]

That is prox_entry(NormL1(λ), t₀ - (2/a) G[r, c], i, 2/a). On the diagonal the factors of 2 disappear and the step is $1/a$. So, as the KronQuadratic example of the D-trace loss shows, off-diagonal coordinates take the step $2/a$ where the diagonal takes $1/a$. Per-entry parameters, such as the weights of NormL1, are indexed like the dense $p \times p$ matrix and must be symmetric.

AtomIterate

An AtomIterate is a vector whose coordinates are groups of entries, partitioning $1{:}p$. Its active groups play the role of the stored entries of a SparseIterate, and atom(x, k) is a view of group $k$. Block coordinate descent for the group lasso takes, for group $k$, a gradient step of size $1/L_k$ (with $L_k = \|X_{G_k}\|_2^2$ for least squares). It then applies the group's proximal step, prox_coordinate!(x, g, k, 1/L_k), which is block soft thresholding, and which activates the group when it ends nonzero. AtomIterate(p, g) builds an iterate that shares the groups of a group penalty g.

Tracking an active set

The active coordinates form a stored working set, which can contain entries or groups that have returned to zero. We keep them during a sweep so that the set being traversed does not change.

OperationMeaning
ncoordinates(x)Number of possible coordinates
nactive(x)Number of stored active coordinates
activecoordinates(x)Indices of the active coordinates
isactive(x, k)Whether coordinate k is active
activate!(x, k)Add a coordinate to the active set
dropzeros!(x)Remove stored zero entries or zero groups

Use kkt_violation to test excluded zero coordinates and restrict to construct a supported restricted problem. The Coordinate descent chapter explains the full optimality checks that must accompany these storage operations.
