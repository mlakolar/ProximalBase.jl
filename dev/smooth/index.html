Linear and quadratic functions

The proximal operator of $f(x) = \langle c, x\rangle + d$ is $x - \gamma c$. For $f(x) = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|x - y\|^2$ the optimality condition $\lambda(z - y) + (z - x)/\gamma = 0$ gives

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x) = \frac{x + \gamma\lambda y}{1 + \gamma\lambda}.\]

Both are separable, so they also have scalar proximal operators for coordinate descent.

For QuadraticFunction, $f(x) = \tfrac12 x^\top A x + b^\top x + c$ with $A$ symmetric positive semidefinite, the proximal point solves $(I + \gamma A) z = x - \gamma b$. The first call computes the eigendecomposition $A = Q\Lambda Q^\top$, once. After that, for any $\gamma$,

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x) = Q\,(I + \gamma\Lambda)^{-1} Q^\top (x - \gamma b)\]

costs two products with $Q$.

The Kronecker quadratic

KronQuadratic is the quadratic of a $p \times q$ matrix $\Theta$

\[f(\Theta) = \tfrac12\langle \Theta, A\Theta B\rangle + \langle C, \Theta\rangle = \tfrac12 \operatorname{vec}(\Theta)^\top (B \otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(\Theta) + \operatorname{vec}(C)^\top\operatorname{vec}(\Theta),\]

for symmetric positive semidefinite $A$ and $B$. The identity uses $\operatorname{vec}(A\Theta B) = (B^\top \otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(\Theta)$. The $pq \times pq$ matrix $B \otimes A$ is never formed. The gradient is $A\Theta B + C$, and since the eigenvalues of $B \otimes A$ are the products of those of $A$ and $B$, $L = \|A\|_2\|B\|_2$.

On a SymmetricSparseIterate the variable is a symmetric matrix, and the gradient is the one over symmetric matrices: the projection of $A\Theta B + C$ onto them. Since $(A\Theta B)^\top = B\Theta A$ for symmetric $\Theta$, it is

\[\tfrac12\,(A\Theta B + B\Theta A) + \tfrac12\,(C + C^\top).\]

A solver that steps along one triangle needs this symmetric gradient.

Example: the D-trace loss. For two covariance matrices $\Sigma_x$ and $\Sigma_y$, the loss $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_x\Delta\Sigma_y\Delta) - \operatorname{tr}(\Delta(\Sigma_y - \Sigma_x))$ (Zhao, Cai and Li, 2014) is KronQuadratic(Σx, Σy, Σx - Σy). Its gradient vanishes at $\Delta = \Sigma_x^{-1} - \Sigma_y^{-1}$, since then $\Sigma_x\Delta\Sigma_y = \Sigma_y - \Sigma_x$. So minimizing it with an ℓ1 penalty estimates the difference of two precision matrices directly; see the differential-network example.

Least squares

For LeastSquaresLoss, $f(\beta) = \tfrac12\|Y - X\beta\|^2$, with $Y$ a vector or a matrix. The gradient is $X^\top(X\beta - Y)$ and $L = \sigma_1(X)^2$. The proximal point solves $(X^\top X + I/\gamma) z = X^\top Y + x/\gamma$. With the thin singular value decomposition $X = U\Sigma V^\top$, computed once, the solution is

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x) = x + V\,\operatorname{diag}\!\Big(\frac{\gamma}{1 + \gamma\sigma_i^2}\Big)\big(\Sigma U^\top Y - \Sigma^2 V^\top x\big),\]

for every $\gamma$. To derive it, split $z$ into its components in and orthogonal to the row space of $X$; the orthogonal component of $x$ passes through unchanged. Each call costs products with $U$ and $V$. That is why ADMM, whose penalty parameter changes, gets the least-squares step at the same cost every iteration.

Generalized linear models

GLMLoss(ℓ, y, X; weights, offset) is the loss $f(\beta) = \sum_i w_i\,\ell(\eta_i, y_i)$ of the linear predictor $\eta = X\beta + o$. With $\ell'$ and $\ell''$ the derivatives in $\eta$,

\[\nabla f(\beta) = X^\top\big(w \odot \ell'(\eta, y)\big), \qquad \nabla^2 f(\beta) = X^\top \operatorname{diag}\big(w \odot \ell''(\eta, y)\big) X \preceq c_\ell\, X^\top W X ,\]

where $c_\ell = \sup \ell''$ is the curvature bound of the scalar loss and $W = \operatorname{diag}(w)$. So $L = c_\ell\|W^{1/2}X\|_2^2$, and lipschitz computes the spectral norm for dense matrices. For sparse and other indexed matrices it uses the conservative bound $\lVert W^{1/2}X\rVert_1\lVert W^{1/2}X\rVert_\infty$ instead of forming $X^\top W X$ or densifying the design. opnorm2 is a separate iterative estimate and needs backtracking or an independent bound when used to select a step. One value and gradient cost one product with $X$, one pass over the observations and one product with $X^\top$.

Here $\sigma(\eta) = 1/(1 + e^{-\eta})$, and $\log(1 + e^\eta)$ is computed so that it neither overflows nor loses precision. The scalar losses expose ProximalBase.deriv, deriv2 and curvature, for Newton and coordinate steps.

SmoothCheck and ConvolutionCheck smooth the check loss $\rho_\tau(u) = \max(\tau u, (\tau - 1)u)$ of quantile regression, so that coordinate descent and proximal Newton fit it without a linear program. SmoothCheck(τ, h) is its Moreau envelope $\min_s \rho_\tau(s) + (s - u)^2/(2h)$: an asymmetric Huber loss that is quadratic on $-h(1 - \tau) < u < h\tau$ and within $h/2$ of $\rho_\tau$. ConvolutionCheck(τ, h) convolves $\rho_\tau$ with a kernel of bandwidth $h$, the smoothing of the conquer estimator (He, Pan, Tan and Zhou, 2023). With the uniform or the Epanechnikov kernel (kernel = :uniform or :epanechnikov) it equals $\rho_\tau$ for $|u| \ge h$ and is a polynomial in between: with $m(s) = \int|s - t|K(t)\,dt$, it is $(\tau - \tfrac12)u + \tfrac{h}{2}m(u/h)$.

The Bernoulli log-likelihood on probabilities

BernoulliLoss(b, w) takes probabilities directly. Its parameters are scalars or arrays with one entry per probability: responses $0 \le b_i \le 1$ and finite weights $w_i \ge 0$, with w = 1 by default. Fractional responses with weights equal to trial counts give the binomial negative log-likelihood. For a positive weight, an endpoint is finite only when $p_i = b_i \in \{0,1\}$, with $0\log 0 = 0$; the value is $+\infty$ elsewhere outside $(0,1)$. A zero weight masks the coordinate, even outside $[0,1]$: its value and gradient are zero and its proximal operator is the identity. NaN inputs propagate. The gradient is NaN outside the domain. The gradient has no global Lipschitz bound. Its coordinate constant is Inf for a positive weight and zero for a masked coordinate; lipschitz(f) is zero only when every weight is zero.

In an identity-link binomial model, $p = X\beta$ and the coefficients give adjusted risk differences. Splitting the graph constraint $p = X\beta$ lets one apply BernoulliLoss(b, w) to $p$ and any penalty to $\beta$. Its proximal operator handles the probability boundary directly, including boundary maximum-likelihood estimates. Applying a shape constraint or a total-variation penalty to $p$ instead fits monotone or piecewise-constant risks. LogisticLoss takes the linear predictor on the logit scale.

For $\mu = \gamma w_i$ and a fractional response, the scalar proximal point is the unique root in $(0,1)$ of

\[p^3 - (1+v)p^2 + (v-\mu)p + \mu b_i = 0.\]

A safeguarded Newton iteration solves the stationarity equation, using $p$ near zero and $1-p$ near one. For $b_i = 1$ the result is the NegLog proximal point clipped at one; $b_i = 0$ gives its reflection, evaluated without cancellation near zero. If a fractional response's result rounds to an endpoint, the nearest interior float in the argument's floating type is returned. A narrower output may still round to the endpoint. value_and_prox! evaluates the stored result.

The Gaussian log-likelihood

LogDetLoss(S, w) is $f(X) = w\,(\operatorname{tr}(SX) - \log\det X)$ on the positive definite matrices and $+\infty$ elsewhere: the loss of the graphical lasso, for a sample covariance $S$. One Cholesky factorization gives its value and its gradient $w(S - X^{-1})$. The value never throws, so a line search can reject a step that leaves the cone. The gradient is not Lipschitz, since $X^{-1}$ blows up at the boundary.

Its proximal operator has a closed form. The optimality condition for $Z = \operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(X)$ is $w(S - Z^{-1}) + (Z - X)/\gamma = 0$, which with $\rho = 1/(\gamma w)$ reads

\[\rho Z - Z^{-1} = \rho X - S .\]

Take the eigendecomposition $\rho X - S = U\operatorname{diag}(t)U^\top$. The matrix $Z = U\operatorname{diag}(\mu)U^\top$ solves the equation when $\rho\mu_i - 1/\mu_i = t_i$, that is, when

\[\mu_i = \frac{t_i + \sqrt{t_i^2 + 4\rho}}{2\rho} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{t_i^2 + 4\rho} - t_i} > 0 .\]

The first form is used for $t_i \ge 0$ and the second for $t_i < 0$, which avoids cancellation. $Z$ is positive definite by construction, and value_and_prox! gets $f(Z)$ from the $\mu_i$. This is the $\Omega$-step of ADMM for the graphical lasso.

Sums

Sum(f1, f2, ...) is $\sum_k f_k$, a smooth function when all the $f_k$ are. Its gradient and its Lipschitz constant are the sums of theirs. A sum has no proximal operator in general.

Second-order models

The smooth functions with hashessian provide two more oracles, for Newton-type methods. hessian_vec!(Hv, f, x, v) computes $\nabla^2 f(x)\,v$ at about the cost of a gradient, and quadratic_model(f, x) returns the second-order model of $f$ at $x$,

\[q(z) = f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), z - x\rangle + \tfrac12\langle z - x, \nabla^2 f(x)(z - x)\rangle \quad (\text{up to a constant}),\]

as another function of ProximalBase. The GLM construction below floors very small observation curvatures, so its model is a regularized curvature model where that floor is active, while preserving the gradient at the model point. Minimizing $q + g$ with any solver and penalty $g$ is a proximal Newton step, and quadratic_model! moves the model to the next point in place.

The quadratics are their own models. For a GLMLoss the model is the weighted least-squares loss of iteratively reweighted least squares,

\[q(\beta) = \tfrac12 \sum_i w_i\,\ell''_i\,\big(\eta_i(\beta) - z_i\big)^2 + \text{const}, \qquad z_i = \eta_i - \frac{\ell'_i}{\ell''_i},\]

with $\ell'_i$ and $\ell''_i$ evaluated at the current linear predictor. The curvature $\ell''_i$ is floored at $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, so that the working responses stay finite where it vanishes (a Huber loss beyond $\delta$); the gradient of $q$ at the current point is still exact.

For LogDetLoss(S, w), with $W = X^{-1}$, the expansion of $w(\operatorname{tr}(SZ) - \log\det Z)$ at $X$ in the step $\Delta = Z - X$ is, up to a constant, $w\operatorname{tr}((S - W)\Delta) + \tfrac{w}{2}\operatorname{tr}(W\Delta W\Delta)$. Written in the new point $Z$, and using $WXW = W$, it is

\[q(Z) = \tfrac{w}{2}\operatorname{tr}(WZWZ) + w\operatorname{tr}\big((S - 2W)Z\big) + \text{const},\]

the KronQuadratic(wW, W, w(S - 2W)). Minimizing $q(Z) + \lambda\|Z\|_1$ over symmetric matrices, by coordinate descent on a SymmetricSparseIterate, is the Newton step of QUIC for the graphical lasso (Hsieh, Sustik, Dhillon and Ravikumar, 2014). Without the penalty, its minimizer is $2X - XSX$, Newton's iteration for $S^{-1}$.
