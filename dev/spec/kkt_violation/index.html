Endpoint clauses

CaseRetained behavior
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), non-finite v or x (NormL1, NormL1Nonneg, ElasticNet, ZeroFunction, LinearFunction, SqrNormL2)The entries of v, and the entries of x that the formula reads, are converted to the working type W first (see the element-types row), and the rules apply to the converted entries. For the entries of v and the entries of x that the formula reads (ZeroFunction and LinearFunction read none of x): a NaN gives NaN; otherwise an infinite entry gives +Inf. In the full-point form a NaN takes precedence over an infinite entry at another position. These rules replace the value of the three-argument screen where that would be -Inf (an infinite NormL1, NormL1Nonneg or ElasticNet coefficient, an infinite ElasticNet ridge level, or v[i] = +Inf for NormL1Nonneg) or NaN (Inf - Inf) for a non-finite v[i], so that a call that reads the point does not report slack or zero where stationarity is undefined
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), finite v and x (after conversion to W), exceptional parametersThe formula is evaluated in floating point in W. For NormL1, NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet, a zero weight gives a zero coefficient at any level, and an infinite coefficient (an infinite weight, an infinite level with a positive weight, or a coefficient whose product overflows in W, as in NormL1(floatmax(Float64), [2.0])) gives -Inf at a zero entry and +Inf at a nonzero entry; the full-point form takes the maximum with 0 over the entries, so a -Inf score of a zero entry does not raise it (the full-point value is 0 when no other entry has a positive score, as in that example). NormL1Nonneg scores a negative entry x[i] +Inf at any coefficient, since the point is outside the domain. An infinite ElasticNet ridge level does the same, and a ridge product λ2 x[i] that overflows at a nonzero entry gives +Inf. For LinearFunction and SqrNormL2 the score is abs(v[i] + c[i]) or abs(v[i] + d[i]) in floating point, so an infinite LinearFunction coefficient gives +Inf (kkt_violation(LinearFunction([Inf]), [0.0], [0.0], 1) is +Inf), a NaN coefficient of a LinearFunction or a NaN center of a SqrNormL2 gives NaN, and SqrNormL2 with an infinite level follows the gradient oracle row above. ZeroFunction has no parameter
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), element typesThe working type W is float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))) for NormL1, NormL1Nonneg, ElasticNet, ZeroFunction and a SqrNormL2 without a center, and float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x), eltype(c))) for a LinearFunction with coefficients c and a SqrNormL2 with a center c (float is applied after the promotion: integer v and x with a Float32 c give Float32). The levels and weights of NormL1, NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet and a scalar SqrNormL2 level do not enter W. The entries of v, and the entries of x that the formula reads, are converted to W before the zero test, the non-finite rules and the formulas, so each statement about a zero, nonzero, finite or infinite entry is about the converted value: kkt_violation(NormL1(0.5), Float16[0], [1//10^12], 1) is -0.5 because the entry converts to zero, and an integer x = [10^6] with a Float16 v converts to Inf and scores +Inf. For NormL1, NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet the coefficient λ w[i] (λ1 w[i] for ElasticNet) is formed by the helper of the three-argument screen and rounded once to W, not as a product of factors rounded to W first (NormL1(1.00000006, [1.00000006]), Float32 v = [0] and x = [1] gives 1.0000001f0, while the product of the two factors rounded to Float32 first gives 1.0000002f0); Bool factors, such as an omitted weight, do not widen the type in which the helper forms it. When eltype(v) is a floating type, the three-argument screen forms its coefficient the same way with W = eltype(v), so with a wider x the coefficient of the point-aware forms can differ from it (NormL1(1/3), Float32 v, Float64 x). For another real element type of v (integers), the three-argument screen keeps the coefficient computed from the level and the weight in their own types (kkt_violation(NormL1(0.5), [0], 1) is the Float64 -0.5, and with NormL1(0.5f0) it is a Float32); the same holds for NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet. The level λ2 of ElasticNet is converted to W with no further range recovery. For finite converted inputs the score of NormL1Nonneg is -v[i] - c[i] at a zero entry, abs(v[i] + c[i]) at a positive entry and Inf at a negative one. Its three-argument screen forms -v[i] - c in P = promote_type(typeof(v[i]), typeof(c)), or in float(P) when P is an unsigned integer type; otherwise the arithmetic of P applies (a signed integer type wraps at typemin). At a nonzero entry the arithmetic is abs(v[i] + c[i] sign(x[i])) for NormL1 and abs((v[i] + λ2 x[i]) + c[i] sign(x[i])) for ElasticNet, with the term λ2 x[i] left out when λ2 is zero. SqrNormL2 converts the level and the center entry to W before forming the gradient entry. For Float32 or Float64 arrays and parameters of one type and a finite v[i], the score at a zero entry equals the three-argument screen bit for bit (a SqrNormL2 with an infinite level excepted). Near a stationary point the terms cancel, so the error of the score need not be small relative to the score
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormL2 and NormL2: element types, zero test, coefficientThe working type W is float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))), and the result has type W in each branch, an entry in no group included (abs of the entry converted to W). The entries of v and x are converted to W before they are classified or measured: a block is zero when all its entries are zeros of either sign after the conversion (-0.0 is zero, a subnormal entry is not), and an entry that does not fit W is infinite there (an integer 10^6 with a Float16 v scores +Inf). The coefficient c = λ0 w[k] (λ w with the weight one for NormL2) is formed by the helper of the three-argument screen and rounded once to W: it is 0 for a zero weight at any level, and Inf for an infinite weight, for an infinite level with a positive weight, and where the product overflows in W. A nonzero block uses the coefficient rounded to W. A zero block reuses the existing screen on v read in W, including the recovery of differences that involve finite out-of-range products; for v, x and the parameters of one floating type and a finite v, its value equals the screen of earlier versions bit for bit
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormL2 and NormL2: non-finite entries and coefficientsIn this order, for the entries of v_G and x_G converted to W: a NaN gives NaN; otherwise an infinite entry gives +Inf. Then, at a nonzero block, an infinite c gives +Inf and c = 0 gives norm(v_G). At a zero block the rules of the screen stay, with one exception: an infinite entry of v_G with an infinite c is +Inf, where the screen is NaN (Inf - Inf). For a finite v_G, an infinite level or weight (with a positive weight) gives -Inf at a zero block; where c overflowed to infinity from finite parameters the screen recovers the difference from the finite factors, and the value can be finite (GroupNormL2(floatmax(Float64), [[1]], [1.5]), v = [floatmax(Float64)] and x = [0.0] give about -floatmax(Float64)/2). The full-point form keeps a NaN in its running maximum, so a NaN takes precedence over +Inf at another group or entry, and takes the maximum with zero of W otherwise (-Inf scores of zero blocks do not raise it). An entry in no group has abs of the converted entry, with NaN giving NaN and an infinite entry +Inf
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormLinf: forms, element types, coefficientThe working type W and the result type are float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))); the entries are converted to W before the zero test (zeros of either sign), and c = λ0 w[k] is formed by the helper of the three-argument screen and rounded once to W (an overflowing product is an infinite coefficient). A zero group has the margin ‖v_G‖₁ - c; a nonzero group the distance from -v_G to the subdifferential of c‖·‖∞ at x_G, by the full-point kernel of NormLinf on the group (the face is decided by equality in W); an entry in no group has abs of the converted entry. The full-point form is the maximum, kept at NaN, of zero, those entries and the distance of every group, where a zero group has its distance to the ℓ₁ ball of radius c (zero for finite entries when the computed ‖v_G‖₁ is at most c) and not its margin; in exact arithmetic, for a positive margin, distance ≤ margin ≤ √n distance. The three-argument form and a SymmetricSparseIterate throw ArgumentError, as does a group of n entries with n + 1 not finite in W (Float16 from n = 65519): a nonzero group in the four-argument form, every group in the full-point form
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormLinf: non-finite entries and coefficientsThe four-argument margin of a zero group, in this order for v_G: a NaN gives NaN; an infinite entry or a sum of magnitudes that overflows gives +Inf, also at an infinite c; then an infinite c gives -Inf, and a zero weight gives ‖v_G‖₁. The full-point term of a zero group follows the zero-point rules of the NormLinf kernel: a NaN gives NaN; then an infinite entry gives +Inf; then c = Inf gives 0 and c = 0 gives ‖v_G‖₂; a finite sum of magnitudes that overflows is not +Inf (the distance is formed in units of a power of two, and is +Inf where it is above floatmax). At a nonzero group, in both forms, for v_G and x_G: a NaN gives NaN; an infinite entry gives +Inf; then an infinite c gives +Inf and c = 0 gives ‖v_G‖₂. In the full-point form a NaN takes precedence over +Inf at another group or entry
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormLinf: accuracyA distance has the first-order bound of the NormLinf accuracy row below for the n entries of the group, (2n + √n + 5) u (norm(v_G) + c) with u = eps(W)/2 and c as rounded to W, for Float32 and Float64 in the ordinary range and assuming n u ≤ 1/4 (unchecked: 512 entries per group in Float16); outside it the value is formed in units of a power of two or recomputed in a wider type, which extends the range and not the precision. The margin is the computed sum of the magnitudes of v_G minus c. In the tests, the Float32 and Float64 calls on arrays allocate nothing after the first call and write neither argument. The call is not tuned for speed
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), GroupNormL2 and NormL2: accuracyIn the ordinary range, which is when the sums of squares of x_G and of the residual are finite and at least n floatmin(W)/eps(W) for a block of n entries and c/norm(x_G) is finite, a nonzero block is computed after the scan for a zero block by two arithmetic passes: the norm of the point (t = c/√Σxⱼ²), then the norm of the residual (√Σ(vⱼ + t xⱼ)²). To first order in n u, with u = eps(W)/2, its error is at most (n + 8) u (norm(v_G) + c), where c is the coefficient as rounded to W. Rounding λ0 w to W adds a relative u for a normal finite product and an absolute term of the subnormal spacing where the product is subnormal or rounds to zero. The bound is relative to the size of the terms that cancel near stationarity, not to the score. The tests use this bound, plus the subnormal spacing of W, for zero blocks as well. The test of the ordinary range is on the sums, not on single entries: a block with a subnormal entry whose sums are ordinary stays on the ordinary path. Outside it (sums that overflow or underflow, a very large c) the block is computed in the recovery type (at least Float64) from the entries read in W, with the norm of x_G and the factor c/norm(x_G) kept as a significand and an exponent: this extends the range of the computation, not its precision, and the result is rounded once to W. The residuals are scaled by the exponent of the largest one so that the accumulated squares do not overflow; a scaled square far below the largest can underflow below the rounding scale of the norm. The Float32 and Float64 calls on arrays allocate nothing in either range (measured by the tests)
kkt_violation(f,v,x), NormLinf: forms, element types, level, faceThe full-point form has a value; the forms for an entry or a group throw an ArgumentError, and so does the full-point form at a SymmetricSparseIterate. The working type W is float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))) and the result has type W (zero(W) for an empty x); a length n for which n + 1 is not a finite number of W (Float16 from n = 65519) throws an ArgumentError before any pass. The entries of v and x are converted to W before they are classified or compared, so each statement about a zero, tied, finite or infinite entry is about the converted value (an entry that does not fit W is infinite there). The level c is λ rounded once to W by the helper of the GroupNormL2 and NormL2 rows with the weight one; a level that overflows in W is an infinite level there. The point is the zero point when all entries of x are zeros of either sign after the conversion (a subnormal entry is not zero). Otherwise the face J is the set of entries whose magnitude equals the largest one, compared for equality in W with no tolerance: an entry one unit in the last place below the largest is not on the face, so for a positive finite level an exact tie can make the value discontinuous in x
kkt_violation(f,v,x), NormLinf: non-finite entries and levelsIn this order, for the entries of v and x converted to W: a NaN gives NaN, also beside an infinite entry in v or in x; otherwise an infinite entry gives +Inf, also where the level is zero or infinite. Then an infinite level gives 0 at the zero point and +Inf at another point, and a zero level gives norm(v), formed as a sum of squares of the entries read in W that is recomputed as below where it is outside the ordinary range. A finite positive level goes to the paths below
kkt_violation(f,v,x), NormLinf: accuracy and rangeThe ordinary path is used when the sum sv of abs(vᵢ) is at most floatmax(W)/2 at the zero point, or (n + 1) sv ≤ floatmax(W)/4 at another point (n = length(x)), the first level of the face, S - c divided by the number of entries of the face (S the sum of -sign(xᵢ) vᵢ over the face), is finite, and the sum of squares Q of the residual is finite and at least n floatmin(W)/eps(W). At the zero point the value is 0 when the computed sv is at most c. Otherwise the level τ at the zero point comes from the iteration of the threshold of the prox (the routine that the prox of NormLinf has as its fallback, with its four returns) on abs(vᵢ), started at the first level (sv - c)/n of the sum sv that the classification formed. At another point the closed form is used when the rounded first level τ0 plus the rounded margin 3u abs(τ0) + 2u cnt maximum(abs, v) is at most the smallest face value -sign(xᵢ) vᵢ, where u = eps(W)/2 and cnt counts the maximal face. In the ordinary range for Float32 and Float64 the comparison certifies that the exact first level is the exact level. A tiny subnormal margin can round to zero and the comparison can accept without certifying exact support. Otherwise the iteration continues from τ0 on the nonnegative shifted values max(-sign(xᵢ) vᵢ - τ0, 0) on the face (and 0 off it), from the level zero, and its level is added to τ0; the computed τ0 can be above or below the exact first level and the exact level. The value is √Q for the residual min(abs(vᵢ), τ) at the zero point, and min(-sign(xᵢ) vᵢ, τ) on the face with vᵢ off it at another point. In the round-to-nearest relative-error model for the ordinary path for Float32 and Float64, assuming n u ≤ 1/4 (unchecked), the absolute error has first-order bound (2n + √n + 5) u (norm(v) + λ) with remainder O_n(u² (norm(v) + λ)) for fixed n. This compares with the exact distance for λ and both inputs read in W and excludes additive subnormal-spacing effects. The signed-face argument interprets rounded shifted entries as exact excesses of perturbed face data, accounting for changes in the active set. The estimate scales with norm(v) + λ. The test tolerance adds 4n nextfloat(zero(W)). Outside the ordinary path the value is formed in units of a power of two. When a sum of magnitudes or the first level is out of range, the level is computed from v and c scaled by 2^-k, with k raised until the scaled sum is in the range of the ordinary path. When Q is not finite or is below n floatmin(W)/eps(W), the residual is read again in the recovery type (at least Float64), scaled by the exponent of its largest entry, and the result is rounded once to W: it is 0 when all the residual entries are zero and Inf for an entry that is not finite. This extends the range of the computation, not its precision: the derivation covers the ordinary path, and the scaling of an entry or of c can lose up to a subnormal spacing of W. The tests compare these paths with the same reference and test tolerance, which they enlarge by a factor of 4 in some cases of overflowing sums, and they do not decide a band of relative width 100 eps(W) around floatmax(W), where the result may be floatmax(W) or Inf; they require Inf where the exact distance is above floatmax(W) by more than that band. Under Scaled with a finite positive factor α the tests accept (2n + √n + 11) u (norm(v) + αλ) against the exact distance for the level αλ, where the added 6 u is an allowance for the rounding of v/α and of the product by α, not a derived constant. The same-type Float32 and Float64 array calls with a level of a hardware float type that the allocation tests use allocate nothing in either range, the first call included, and write neither v, x nor the buffer of the object (measured by the tests); wider parameter arithmetic such as a BigFloat level can allocate
kkt_violation(f,v,x), LeastSquaresLoss and QuadraticFunctionThe gradient at x is written into scratch of the function's floating type T. Its entries and those of v are added in float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x), T)), then the largest absolute residual is returned. A NaN in v or x gives NaN, otherwise an infinite input entry gives +Inf. With finite inputs the result follows ordinary gradient and residual arithmetic, including overflow and NaN. See smooth stationarity scores
kkt_violation(f,v,x,k) and kkt_violation(f,v,x), ScaledFor a finite positive α the call evaluates the existing score of the function on the lazy gradient v/α, with the rounding and range limits of that evaluation (the leaf's gradient entry or its sums can overflow or underflow although 1/α and v[i]/α do not), and multiplies it by α; underflow, overflow and NaN are not recovered. The reciprocal of the factor takes part in the type promotion (an Int factor introduces Float64; BigFloat data stay BigFloat). Nested wrappers whose product of factors is not a normal number, an infinite factor, and the functions without a score are listed in the scaling guide. A stack that nests an infinite factor with another Scaled wrapper (a direct call of the parameterized constructor Scaled{F,T} builds it; Scaled(f, α) merges the factors) has unspecified three-argument and zero-coordinate values and throws an ArgumentError at a nonzero coordinate or group and in the full-point form

References to the squared-norm “oracle row above” refer to SqrNormL2 endpoint clauses.
