KronQuadratic numerical contract

Identity

KronQuadratic is the Kronecker-structured quadratic of a matrix argument. Its constructor and oracles are implemented in src/functions/kronquadratic.jl.

Definition and domain

KronQuadratic(A, B, C = nothing) represents $q(X)=\frac12\langle X,AXB\rangle_F+\langle C,X\rangle_F$ for a $p\times q$ matrix $X$, with $C=0$ when it is omitted. This equals $\frac12\operatorname{vec}(X)^\top(B\otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(X)+\operatorname{vec}(C)^\top\operatorname{vec}(X)$.

A is $p\times p$ and B is $q\times q$ with $p,q\geq1$. Both are finite and exactly symmetric: mirrored entries compare equal, or the argument is a Symmetric, Hermitian or Diagonal wrapper. C is a finite $p\times q$ matrix. A, B and C have one element type T, Float32 or Float64. The constructor stores dense copies; later changes to the caller's arrays do not change the function.

The gradient over $p\times q$ matrices is $AXB+C$. Over symmetric $p\times p$ matrices with the Frobenius inner product it is $\frac12(AXB+BXA)+C$ for a symmetric C.

Traits

The function is smooth. It is not proximable or separable, and isconvex is false. The function is convex when A and B are both positive semidefinite or both negative semidefinite; the constructor does not check semidefiniteness. The exact product $\|A\|_2\|B\|_2$ of the spectral norms is a Lipschitz constant of the gradient; lipschitz returns the product of the two computed norms in working precision, which can round below the exact product and is subject to the ordinary range limits: it can overflow to Inf, or be NaN when one norm overflows and the other is zero. size(f) is $(p, q)$.

Supported arguments

A matrix argument is a one-based AbstractMatrix{T} of size $(p, q)$: dense matrices, strided views, SparseIterate, Symmetric wrappers and matrixview of a symmetric iterate. The products read the argument directly when it is a StridedMatrix with first stride 1 and a column stride of at least $\max(1, p)$; any other matrix argument, reversed columns included, is copied into the function's scratch first.

A SymmetricSparseIterate argument has element type T and dimension $p$, and requires $p=q$.

gradient! and value_and_gradient! write into a one-based writable StridedMatrix{T} of size $(p, q)$, row-strided views included. With a matrix argument, a destination that is not a StridedMatrix with first stride 1 and column stride of at least $\max(1, p)$ receives a copy of the result from scratch; with a symmetric iterate, the destination is written entry by entry. gradient returns a Matrix{T}, and value_and_gradient returns (v, G) with v::T and G::Matrix{T}. Vector arguments, BlockIterate, symmetric-iterate destinations and proximal operators are unsupported.

Preconditions

The matrices that ProximalBase.kronquadratic_data returns are the stored copies, ordinary mutable matrices; treat them as read-only. The oracles write scratch matrices stored in the function: calls on one object must not run concurrently. Use one object per task. The destination must be independent of the argument and of the matrices kronquadratic_data returns. As for the other built-in gradients (gradient!), G === X throws ArgumentError and other overlapping views are unsupported; the failure behavior lists the overlaps that the oracles detect.

Results

The value has type T. For a matrix argument the gradient is $AXB+C$. For a symmetric iterate it is $\frac12(W+W^\top)+C$ with $W=AXB$, computed as $W/2+W^\top/2+C$. The two entries of a mirrored pair add the same two halves and mirrored entries of C: they compare equal when the result is not NaN, a NaN occurs in both mirrored positions, and their signs of zero can differ where those of C do; issymmetric holds for results without NaN. When every operation stays finite and in the normal range, halving before the sum gives the rounded $(W+W^\top)/2$. In the subnormal range a nonzero half can round to zero or lose digits, which is ordinary IEEE arithmetic, and $W_{ij}+W_{ji}$ can overflow where the halves and their sum do not. Its off-diagonal entry $(r,c)$ is half the derivative of $q$ along the coordinate $(r,c)$, which moves both mirrored entries. The value is the same formula for both arguments.

Ordinary-path clauses

For a matrix argument, the oracles form $AX$ and $(AX)B$ with mul!, which uses BLAS for eligible layouts and sizes, and the value as $\langle X,AXB\rangle/2+\langle C,X\rangle$. At a symmetric iterate with $n$ nonzero active values, gradient and value-only calls take the accumulating route when $32n\leq p^2$ and the dense route otherwise. On the accumulating route, a gradient call accumulates $AX$ over the active coordinates, and a value-only call accumulates $AX$ and $BX$ and sums $\sum_{ij}(AX)_{ij}(BX)_{ji}$ in tiles of up to $32\times32$ entries, without forming $(AX)B$. A zero coefficient, stored or absent, contributes nothing to that accumulation. On the dense route $AX$ is formed by mul! on a dense copy. A gradient call then forms $(AX)B$ with mul! and writes $W/2+W^\top/2+C$ with $W=(AX)B$ in column order; on the accumulating route its value is a pass over the stored entries. The cutoff is a measured tradeoff near the crossover: in exclusive sweeps on separate nodes of a single x86-64 processor model with Julia 1.10.13 and 1.13.4, for $p\geq200$ and below the cutoff, the accumulating routes ran at 0.045–0.835 (value-only calls) and 0.511–0.881 (gradient calls) of the time of a straightforward dense implementation, at least 1.2× faster except in the last cells before the cutoff at $p=800$, and the dense routes within 1.012 of it. The measured performance section of benchmark/README.md names those cells and gives the numbers per size. Results are rounded in working precision, and the routes can differ by rounding.

Range clauses

Values and gradients use ordinary working-precision arithmetic, without the range recovery that the shared range guarantees describe for other quadratic values. The selected kernel rounds its operations in T; the accumulating kernels use muladd, and BLAS kernels can also fuse a multiplication with an addition and round once. A result can be subnormal, zero or nonfinite where the exact value is not, and the outcome depends on the route and on the order of the operations. For example, the accumulating symmetric routes add the two mirrored entries of C before multiplying by the coefficient, which can overflow where the dense route does not.

Endpoint clauses

NaN and infinite entries of a matrix argument propagate by IEEE arithmetic. At a symmetric iterate on the accumulating route, zero coefficients are skipped; the subsequent products follow IEEE arithmetic. An empty symmetric iterate has value zero and gradient C (zero when C is omitted).

Failure behavior

The constructor throws ArgumentError for element types other than one common Float32 or Float64, with a suggested conversion of each mismatching argument to one of the two precisions, such as Float64.(C) when A and B are Float64 matrices; for empty A or B; for non-finite entries; for offset axes; and for an A or B that is not exactly symmetric, with the message of the shared symmetry check. A nonsquare A or B, or a C of the wrong size, throws DimensionMismatch.

The oracles check, before any write, the element types of the argument and the destination (ArgumentError), one-based axes (ArgumentError), sizes (DimensionMismatch), a strided destination (ArgumentError), G === X and an overlap of the destination with the argument, A, B or C that Base.mightalias reports (ArgumentError), and at a symmetric iterate $p=q$ (DimensionMismatch) and, when C is supplied, its exact symmetry (ArgumentError). C is not averaged.

Limitations and non-guarantees

There is no proximal operator, Hessian-vector product, coordinate curvature or restrict. Convexity is not certified. Overflow and underflow are not recovered. Overlapping views that Base.mightalias does not report are not detected. The summation order depends on the route: BLAS kernels where mul! uses them; the column accumulation of the accumulating route; the tiled sum of its value-only call; and Julia's dot or a pass over the stored entries for the other values.

Evidence
