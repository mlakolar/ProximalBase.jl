Range clauses

The ElasticNet range clauses explain the Julia 1.10 subnormal-scaling allowance cited here.

Reference level for the rounded radius

The clip level τ of NormLinf has an absolute error. On the ordinary finite path, for n ≥ 1 entries in the floating type T of the output with n eps(T) ≤ 1/2 (up to 2^22 entries in Float32 and 2^51 in Float64), a hypothesis that the code does not check, the level returned by the threshold satisfies |τ - τ*| ≤ 2.25 n eps(T) max|xᵢ| + 2nδ. When the rounded radius is positive, τ* is the unique real root of Σ(|xᵢ| - τ)₊ = a for the entries abs(convert(T, xᵢ)) and the radius a that the prox forms (the product γλ rounded in the promoted type of γ, λ and T, then to T), and δ = nextfloat(zero(T)) (the storage of a subnormal radius or output adds δ; x = [δ], λ = δ, γ = 0.5 gives [δ] where the exact point is δ/2). The positive-radius reference can be negative. The prox clips at the larger of the level and zero, which is within the same bound of max(τ*, 0).

If the radius rounds to zero, every level at or above the largest input magnitude solves the equation. The reference uses the smallest of these levels, the largest magnitude in the working type. Clipping at that reference would leave the represented input unchanged.

Threshold derivation and scaling

The derivation, in src/functions/normlinf.jl, bounds the excess Σ(|xᵢ| - τ)₊ - a for each of the four ways the threshold search ends, and it does not assume that the entries it found above the level are the entries above the exact level. Unlike the relative bounds of the NormL2 and GroupNormL2 kernels, the bound is absolute: an entry far below max|xᵢ| is not clipped to a relative accuracy. A sum of magnitudes that overflows or exceeds floatmax(T)/2 is processed in power-of-two units: the statement above then applies first to the scaled entries and the scaled radius, as they are rounded, and converting back carries the scaling allowances described here (the radius γλ rounded to T can itself be infinite there, as for x = [M, M], λ = 1.5, γ = M with M = floatmax(T), which is not the reference of the bound). The subnormal losses of the scaling are below the nominal scale n eps(T) max|xᵢ| under the same hypothesis on n, and the scaling of an entry or of the radius changes it by at most one subnormal spacing (the allowance covers the ldexp of Julia 1.10 noted above), a second hypothesis of that argument that the code does not check. Through Scaled, the wrapper's recovery of a radius γλα beyond the range has a limit: for Scaled(NormLinf(2.0 * 2.0^-1000), 2.0^1000) at the step 0.75 floatmax(Float64) on two entries equal to floatmax(Float64), even the folded level at half the scale is out of range, the wrapper passes an infinite step, and the output is +0 where the exact result is about floatmax/4. For positive finite level and step, an infinite entry without NaN gives the identity; on the finite clipping branch the value is λ τ, computed from the largest stored magnitude τ, and value_and_prox! returns the value of the stored output, computed as f(y) computes it. Endpoint precedence is given in the table below.

The error of $\tau$ is absolute: on the ordinary finite path, at most $2.25\,n\,\varepsilon\max_i\lvert x_i\rvert+2n\delta$ for $n\ge1$ entries with $n\varepsilon\le 1/2$, relative to the radius $a$ that the proximal operator forms from $\gamma$ and $\lambda$ and the entries read in the output type, with $\varepsilon$ the machine epsilon and $\delta$ the smallest subnormal number of the output type (the storage of a subnormal radius or output adds $\delta$). The numerical contract states the hypotheses and the scaled units of the exceptional path.

This passage concerns full-point optimality scores.

For finite entries, a zero level gives $\lVert v\rVert_2$, and an infinite level gives 0 at the zero point and Inf at another point.

Non-finite full-point scores

A NaN entry of v or x gives NaN, also beside an infinite entry; otherwise an infinite entry gives Inf, also at a zero or an infinite level.

Working type and range

The working type is float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))). The rules for the levels, the non-finite entries, the ranges and the accuracy are in the numerical contract.
