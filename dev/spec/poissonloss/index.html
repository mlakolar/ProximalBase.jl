PoissonLoss numerical contract

Identity

PoissonLoss is the Poisson regression loss with the log link on dense Float64 data. Its constructor and oracles are implemented in src/functions/poissonloss.jl. The API coverage matrix lists the supported interface.

Definition and domain

PoissonLoss(y, X) takes a response vector y of length $n$ and a design $X$ with $n$ rows. With $\eta = X\beta$, the mathematical value and gradient are

\[f(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^n \bigl(e^{\eta_i}-y_i\eta_i\bigr),\qquad \nabla f(\beta)=X^\top d,\quad d_i=e^{\eta_i}-y_i.\]

The constant $\log\Gamma(y_i+1)$ of the negative Poisson log-likelihood is omitted. Responses are finite and nonnegative; noninteger responses are accepted, and -0.0 is a zero response.

The value is the sum over observations. For $n>0$ and a finite sum, (1/n) * f, that is Scaled(f, 1/n), gives the mean as the computed sum scaled by 1/n with ordinary rounding. For $n=0$, evaluating (1/0) * f returns NaN. A sum that overflows to Inf stays Inf after scaling by 1/n.

The constructor has no weight or offset argument. The predictor oracles evaluate the loss at a supplied predictor that meets the requirements under Supported arguments and Preconditions. At a supplied $\eta = X\beta + \mathrm{offset}$ they give the value $\sum_i (e^{\eta_i}-y_i\eta_i)$, the derivative $d_i=e^{\eta_i}-y_i$ and the gradient $X^\top d$ of the model with that offset. An exposure $t_i$ enters as the offset $\log t_i$.

Traits

isconvex and issmooth return true; isproximable and isseparable return false. The API supplies no gradient Lipschitz bound: lipschitz(f) throws ArgumentError.

Supported arguments

The constructor takes y::Vector{Float64} and X::Matrix{Float64}. Coefficients and gradient outputs of length size(X, 2), and predictors and derivative outputs of length length(y), are one-based StridedVector{Float64}. The operations are f(β), gradient!(g, f, β), value_and_gradient!(g, f, β), ProximalBase.value_from_predictor(f, η), ProximalBase.value_and_gradient_from_predictor!(g, d, f, η) and the allocating gradient and value_and_gradient. A loss wrapped in Scaled supports f(β), gradient! and value_and_gradient!; the two predictor oracles take an unwrapped loss.

Preconditions

The constructor borrows y and X. Each call reads the current entries of y, and each call that uses the design reads the current entries of X. An in-place edit must keep the responses finite and nonnegative; this is not checked after construction. A change of length needs a new loss.

The calls f(β), gradient! and value_and_gradient! write a private scratch vector and need synchronization across tasks. The predictor oracles write no field of the loss. Concurrent predictor calls on one loss need y, X and every shared read buffer to stay unchanged during the calls, and each written output to be disjoint from the inputs and outputs of every other concurrent call.

Before any write, a call with otherwise valid arguments throws ArgumentError when Base.mightalias reports an overlap of two nonempty arrays: an output and the input, the other output, y, X or the scratch, or a coefficient vector or predictor and the scratch. Sharing that Base.mightalias does not report is the caller's responsibility.

Results

f(β) returns the value at $\eta = X\beta$. gradient! writes $X^\top d$ to g and returns g. value_and_gradient! writes $X^\top d$ to g and returns the value. value_from_predictor returns the value at the supplied predictor. value_and_gradient_from_predictor! writes $d_i=e^{\eta_i}-y_i$ to d and $X^\top d$ to g, and returns the value. The predictor oracles do not form X*β; the fused oracle forms one transposed product X'*d when both dimensions of X are nonzero and none otherwise. The supplied predictor is not modified.

Ordinary-path clauses

The bounds below are relative to the predictor that the loop reads: the computed X*β for f(β), gradient! and value_and_gradient!, and the supplied predictor for the predictor oracles. They do not include the rounding of X*β. They assume round-to-nearest arithmetic with gradual underflow. Let eps = eps(Float64) and let $e_i = e^{\eta_i}$ be the exact exponential. The term and derivative bounds hold for an observation whose computed Float64 values exp(ηᵢ) and yᵢ*ηᵢ are both finite.

  • Each computed value term differs from $e_i-y_i\eta_i$ by at most $4\,\mathrm{eps}\,(e_i+\lvert y_i\eta_i\rvert)+2^{-1074}$.
  • Each computed derivative $\hat d_i$ differs from $d_i=e_i-y_i$ by at most $4\,\mathrm{eps}\,(e_i+y_i)+2^{-1074}$.
  • When every computed term $\hat v_i$ and every partial sum of the value is finite, the computed value differs from $\sum_i\hat v_i$ by at most $\gamma_{n-1}\sum_i\lvert\hat v_i\rvert$, with $\gamma_k=(k\,\mathrm{eps}/2)/(1-k\,\mathrm{eps}/2)$. An empty sum is zero.
  • When every $\hat d_i$, every product $X_{ij}\hat d_i$ and every partial sum of entry $j$ of the transposed product is finite, entry $j$ of the gradient output differs from $(X^\top d)_j$ by at most $\sum_i\lvert X_{ij}\rvert\,\lvert\hat d_i-d_i\rvert+\gamma_n\sum_i\lvert X_{ij}\rvert\,\lvert\hat d_i\rvert$ plus $2^{-1075}$ for each nonzero exact product $X_{ij}\hat d_i$ whose computed value is subnormal or zero, for any summation order of the transposed product.

The bounds are absolute. There is no relative-accuracy statement for a value term or derivative whose two parts nearly cancel, or for a value whose terms cancel across observations. Julia documents no ulp bound for exp; the constant four includes a margin for it.

Range clauses

exp(ηᵢ) is finite at $\eta_i=\log(\texttt{floatmax(Float64)})\approx 709.7827$ and overflows for a finite predictor above it. When exp(ηᵢ) overflows, the value term and the derivative are Inf, also when yᵢ*ηᵢ overflows. When exp(ηᵢ) is finite and yᵢ*ηᵢ overflows, the value term is -Inf for $\eta_i>0$ and Inf for $\eta_i<0$, and the derivative is finite. The loss does not recover a finite exact term or derivative whose computation overflows. The exact value term can lie in the finite Float64 range for $\eta_i$ up to about 716.4, and the exact derivative for $\eta_i$ up to about 710.48. The sum of the terms and the transposed product use ordinary floating-point arithmetic with their usual rounding and range limits.

Implementation notes (not guarantees). One loop computes the requested quantities from e = exp(η): the value term v = ifelse(iszero(y) | (e == Inf), e, e - y*η) and the derivative d = e - y, with plain accumulation and no @simd, muladd or fma.

Endpoint clauses

At η = -Inf, a zero response gives value term 0 and derivative 0, and a positive response gives value term Inf and derivative -y. At η = Inf, the value term and the derivative are Inf. A NaN predictor gives a NaN value term and a NaN derivative. A response of -0.0 follows the zero-response cases. At a finite predictor, no value term or derivative is NaN. Nonfinite entries of a coefficient vector or predictor are not refused.

Failure behavior

The constructor throws DimensionMismatch when length(y) != size(X, 1) and ArgumentError for a negative, NaN or infinite response. A weights or offset keyword, other array or element types, views and sparse designs throw MethodError; a non-one-based y or X throws ArgumentError or MethodError.

An operation throws DimensionMismatch for an argument of the wrong length, MethodError or ArgumentError for an argument of another element type or a non-one-based argument, and ArgumentError for an overlap under Preconditions. These checks run before the call writes, so the outputs and the scratch are unchanged after a refusal. An argument with several faults gets one of the corresponding exceptions. A Scaled loss runs the checks of the loss before it writes. The predictor oracles throw MethodError for a Scaled loss.

Limitations and non-guarantees

The loss does not recover a finite exact term or derivative whose computation overflows (see Range clauses). Aggregates of finite inputs can be nonfinite: the value is NaN when one term is Inf and another -Inf; an entry of the transposed product can be NaN where an infinite $d_i$ meets $X_{ij}=0$ or where infinite contributions of both signs meet; f(β) with finite X and β can form an infinite predictor; and a partial sum of the value or of the transposed product that overflows is not recovered, also when the exact result is finite.

The loss has no observation weights, no offset field and no second-order interface, and the API supplies no gradient Lipschitz bound.

Evidence

test/poissonloss.jl checks the following cases.

  • Value terms, derivatives, value and gradient against a 2048-bit BigFloat evaluation at the Float64 predictor, within the bounds above: six sets with predictors from -1000.0 to 700.0 and responses from 0.0 to 1e12, including zero responses at -745.0 and -745.2; the predictor $\log(\texttt{floatmax(Float64)})$ with response 1.0; a caller-formed exposure offset; data edited after construction; and a design entry nextfloat(0.0) with derivative 0.5, whose product rounds to zero.
  • The subnormal derivative of a zero response at -745.0, written to the gradient.
  • Value terms and derivatives at η = -Inf and Inf with responses 0.0, -0.0 and 2.0; at a NaN predictor with responses 0.0 and 2.0; at nextfloat(log(floatmax(Float64))) and 800.0 with response 1.0; and at (η, y) equal to (709.0, 2.6e305), (-2.0, floatmax(Float64)), (800.0, floatmax(Float64)) and (800.0, 1e300). Each case runs through value_from_predictor, the fused oracle, f(β), value_and_gradient and gradient.
  • A finite value and gradient at $\log(\texttt{floatmax(Float64)})$.
  • Aggregates of finite inputs: a NaN value from terms Inf and -Inf; a NaN gradient entry from an infinite derivative and a zero design entry; a NaN gradient entry from infinite derivatives meeting design entries 1.0 and -1.0 in one column; an Inf value from an overflowing partial sum; a nonfinite gradient entry from overflowing products with a zero exact gradient; and an Inf value from an overflowing matrix product.
  • Central finite differences of f(β) against the gradient at one point.
  • At one point: equal values from f(β), value_and_gradient, value_and_gradient! and the two predictor oracles; the gradients of gradient, value_and_gradient and the fused oracle within the bounds above; and the predictor oracles leaving the scratch and the predictor unchanged.
  • Constructor refusals, and acceptance of the responses -0.0 and 1e300.
  • Refused calls for wrong lengths, element types and axes, and for scratch and output overlaps, each followed by a check that the outputs, the scratch, y, X, β, η and each array argument of the call are unchanged.
  • Scaled with factors 1.75 and 1/5, and MethodError from the predictor oracles for a Scaled loss.
  • In-place edits of y and X seen by the next call, and strided views for β, g, η and d.
  • Empty shapes (0, 3), (2, 0) and (0, 0).
  • Traits and the lipschitz refusal.
  • Zero warmed allocations of the five operations at one point.
  • Product counts of the five operations, and no products from f(β), gradient!, value_and_gradient! and the fused oracle for empty designs.
