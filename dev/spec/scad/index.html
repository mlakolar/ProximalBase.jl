SCAD numerical contract

Identity

SCAD is the separable smoothly clipped absolute deviation penalty. Its scalar hooks are defined in src/functions/scad.jl.

Definition and domain

SCAD(λ, a=3.7) takes a nonnegative real level and a real shape above 2. Both parameters can be positive infinity. For a positive finite level and finite shape, the penalty sums the following contribution at each magnitude $t=|x_i|$.

\[p(t) = \begin{cases} \lambda t & 0 \le t \le \lambda, \\ \lambda t - (t-\lambda)^2 / (2(a-1)) & \lambda < t < a\lambda, \\ (a+1)\lambda^2/2 & t \ge a\lambda. \end{cases}\]

Traits

The family is separable, proximable and zero-preserving. Its type-level convexity and smoothness traits are false. Restriction returns the same function because the parameters are shared across entries.

Supported arguments

Direct value and proximal oracles use the real, one-based dense array and iterate routes of SeparableFunction. The numerical targets are homogeneous CPU Float32 and Float64. The one-argument constructor promotes the level with the Float64 default shape. The omitted unit step also uses Float64 working precision for Float32 inputs, even when both parameters are Float32. For the homogeneous Float32 path on a Float32 array x, pass both parameters and the step explicitly.

f = SCAD(0.5f0, 3.7f0)
y = prox(f, x, 1f0)

Array steps pair with coordinates by linear index on dense arrays, SparseIterate and BlockIterate. A SymmetricSparseIterate destination requires a scalar step.

Preconditions

Exact input/output identity and independent destinations are supported. Sizes and axes follow the array conventions. Use nonnegative, non-NaN steps with prox_block and as the effective steps passed through Scaled.

Results

prox allocates the result and prox! returns its destination. value_and_prox! evaluates the value at the stored result, including destination rounding. Sparse value and same-kind sparse proximal routes visit stored entries. The symmetric value counts each off-diagonal entry twice. Block proximal calls visit the full array.

Ordinary-path clauses

For a positive finite level and step, and a finite shape and input, the scalar operator minimizes $p(u) + (u-|v|)^2/(2\gamma)$ over magnitudes $u \ge 0$. The middle interval has positive, zero or negative curvature as the step passes through $a-1$. Its interior minimum is eligible when the curvature is positive. In the other cases its endpoints suffice, with the smaller endpoint representing a flat interval. The proximal operator selects the smaller magnitude on an exact tie. A positive magnitude takes the sign of the input.

Range clauses

The comparisons for large steps use algebraic differences of the complete objective in magnitude units. This avoids forming the squared distance and the penalty plateau when either would leave the floating-point range. Power-of-two scaling protects the comparison with a zero candidate when the squared cutoff overflows, and the join comparison at tiny magnitudes. For an overflowing upper-join residual, the middle root can be formed from the soft-threshold point. The value sums contributions in the result type.

Endpoint clauses

Apply the first matching proximal rule for a valid step.

Level and stepProximal entry
λ = 0Identity, including at γ = Inf
λ = InfPositive zero, including at γ = 0
0 < λ < Inf, γ = 0Identity
0 < λ < Inf, γ = InfPositive zero
0 < λ < Inf, 0 < γ < InfGlobal minimizer for finite input. NaN and signed infinities pass through

Identity routes preserve signed zero. A zero minimizer selected by thresholding or projection is positive zero. At a zero level the value is zero for every input. At a positive level a NaN gives a NaN value. At an infinite level the value is zero at either signed zero and infinite at every nonzero numeric input. At a positive finite level and finite shape, the value at either infinity is the plateau, which can exceed the result type's range.

At a positive finite level and infinite shape the value is the ℓ₁ value. The proximal operator at a positive finite step is soft thresholding. At a fixed magnitude the middle piece tends to the linear value as the shape increases. As the level increases, each nonzero fixed magnitude eventually lies on the first piece and its value diverges. The infinite-level limit is therefore the indicator of zero.

Failure behavior

The constructor rejects negative or NaN levels and shapes at or below 2 or equal to NaN with ArgumentError. Direct array oracles reject negative or NaN steps before writes. They check every entry of an array step, including inactive coordinates. Array steps on symmetric sparse outputs are rejected before writes. Structural checks follow the array conventions.

Limitations and non-guarantees

Finite comparisons near a change of minimizer can round. The homogeneous targets describe the supported precision. Positive Scaled factors use the wrapper's effective step and value rules. A zero factor throws at construction. An infinite factor at a positive step can give a NaN value at zero output. An infinite factor at zero step gives an effective step outside the supported domain.
