Range clauses

The definitions of u and the boundary regions referred to below are in the least-squares predictor range discussion.

Accuracy and range. The value is never formed as $\sqrt{2\,v_{\text{LS}}}$ from the least-squares value $v_{\text{LS}}$, whose squares overflow where the norm does not. For strided Float32 or Float64 vectors of one element type, which covers both oracles, the sum of squares $s$ is formed in T in one vectorizing loop up to $2^{17}$ entries and by pairwise summation above. The other arguments of f(β), such as mixed element types or non-strided arrays, take generic sums in one loop at every length. The product $\alpha\sqrt s$ is kept when $s$ is not below $n\,\texttt{floatmin}(T)/\varepsilon_T$ and the product is below $\texttt{floatmax}(T)/2$ (a NaN or infinite $s$ is not kept). Otherwise, and for every sum that is not finite, the norm is recomputed from the squares in units of a power of two, with $\alpha$ folded in and the squares summed compensated, in at least Float64. For finite entries that T represents exactly, $n\le 2^{40}$ of them, with $(n+3)\varepsilon_T\le 1/16$ or strided Float32 or Float64 vectors of one element type, and $v$ the exact value, the result is within $(n/2+4)\,\varepsilon_T|v|$ of $v$, plus two units of the smallest subnormal where $|v|<\texttt{floatmin}(T)$; with $u$ and $w=(n/2+4)\varepsilon_T$ as in the range statement above, the result is finite where $|v|(1+w)\le\texttt{floatmax}(T)$, is Inf above $(\texttt{floatmax}(T)+u/2)(1+w)$, and between the two is Inf or finite within the bound (floatmax(T) is possible within $w$ of the threshold, below it as well as above it). The proof is in the comments of src/functions/sqrt_leastsquares.jl. f(β) returns its value converted to float(eltype(β)), so the bound holds for eltype(β) == T, and a narrower β adds one rounding to its type. An entry that T does not represent exactly (an Int Y above maxintfloat(T), a Rational Y) is rounded to T in the sum, outside the bound. An exact fit gives +0; a NaN entry gives NaN; an entry that is ±Inf gives Inf, unless it meets an Inf of the same sign in z and Y, where the difference is NaN.

Endpoint clauses

An entry of $X\beta$ that overflows is not formed again, unlike LeastSquaresLoss: the value is then the norm of the computed $X\beta-Y$, typically Inf or NaN.

Limitations and non-guarantees

The wrapper. Scaled(f, α)(β) is the generic wrapper. It receives f(β) already rounded to float(eltype(β)), forms the product with $\alpha$ from that rounded value and returns it converted to float(eltype(β)). A β narrower than T therefore rounds the inner value to its own type first: with f = SqrtLeastSquaresLoss([0.0], fill(0.25, 1, 1)), β = Float32[nextfloat(0f0)] and α = 4.0, f(β) and Scaled(f, α)(β) are 0.0f0, while the oracle at X * β is about 1.4e-45. For eltype(β) == T, with a Float64 factor on Float32 data the result can differ from the oracle in the last bit, and where the oracle recovers a value that the wrapper's product loses it can differ on the exceptional range (issue #609, as for the least-squares loss). Scaled(f, Inf)(β) is Inf * f(β), which is NaN at an exact fit; the oracles refuse the factor Inf.
