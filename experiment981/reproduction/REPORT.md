# ProximalBase PR #981 experimental comparison

**C, the sorted-prefix projection, is the winner for the Float32 and Float64 score. A, shared-kernel reuse, is the runner-up.** C was the only candidate to preserve stationarity decisions on the added cancellation tests and correctly return the representable mixed-scale subnormal scores. Its pinned implementation has a BigFloat face-membership regression and a Float16 norm regression. The comparison supports using C as the reference and as the preferred design for further integration work. All three pins require correctness work before integration.

## Projection methods

At zero, the residual is the Euclidean distance from `v` to the ℓ₁ ball of radius `c`. Outside the ball, its components have magnitudes `min(abs(vᵢ), τ)`, where `Σ max(abs(vᵢ) − τ, 0) = c`. At a nonzero point, define the maximal face `J` using exact magnitude equality after working-type conversion. For `uᵢ = −sign(xᵢ)vᵢ` on this face, project onto the nonnegative simplex with mass `c`. Solve `Σ_{i∈J} max(uᵢ − τ, 0) = c`, allowing negative `τ`. The squared distance sums `min(uᵢ, τ)²` on the face and `vᵢ²` off it.

- **A** reuses the corrected shared `_l1ball_iterate` for the ball. On a face it checks an all-active certificate, then otherwise starts the shared iteration at zero on the lazy entries `max(uᵢ − σ, 0)`, with `σ` the computed all-face first level. In exact arithmetic that shift is a lower bound and the shifted equation is equivalent. If rounding puts the shift above the root, the shifted mass can lie below the ball radius. The shared routine can return zero at that starting level, leaving the shift error in the face threshold.
- **B** reuses the shared ball iteration and solves the signed-face equation directly. The largest entry minus the radius is a lower bound. The smallest entry gives the boundary of the all-active case. The solver either takes the all-active step below that boundary or moves upward with residual-form Newton steps. Each pass visits the whole input.
- **C** sorts the eligible entries and uses compensated prefix sums. In descending order, a prefix of length `k` stops when the next entry is at most `(Sₖ − c)/k`. Its active residuals are equal, so their norm is `abs(Sₖ − c)/sqrt(k)`. This evaluates the aggregate before rounding the scalar threshold near underflow. C handles a singleton face directly and combines the active, inactive, and off-face contributions with Euclidean norms.

The near-overflow `huge_zero` test sets the common maximum absolute errors at approximately `5.07e30` (Float32) and `4.99e291` (Float64) for all three. Including subnormal cases, the maximum normalized common-suite error is about `0.04006` for each, from a zero-point case within two subnormal spacings. B’s aggregate subnormal failure misses almost 32 spacings, despite its smaller normalized error of `0.015625`. The independent checks below give separate absolute errors for stationarity decisions.

The performance figures are diagnostic because all three pins failed at least one correctness check. Discovery job 14362078 completed in 3 minutes 9 seconds on exclusive node `d11-37`, an Intel Xeon Silver 4116. It used one bound core, eight alternating repetitions, and 140 paired cells per comparison and Julia version. The dimensions were 2, 8, 32, 256, 4,096, 100,000, and 1,000,000. Typical ratios are medians of per-cell paired ratios over 112 ordinary cells per version. A/A absolute median-ratio deviations were `0.18% / 0.23%` at the median and `0.61% / 0.78%` at the 90th percentile. An isolated 15.3% A/A deviation occurred in an approximately 20 ns Julia 1.13.4 cell. The larger reported effects repeat across versions.

C sorts even on ordinary points already inside the ball, which accounts for its largest slowdown. A and B can return after classifying the inputs. C is competitive on many small faces because it sorts only the tied coordinates. B’s large-face slowdown occurs despite fewer input traversal equivalents in the separate instrumentation. Its inner loop uses a signed-value test and an explicit next-breakpoint reduction. The run measures the combined cost and leaves the per-operation contribution unmeasured. First public calls after source loading took roughly 1.50–1.78 s for A, 0.57–0.67 s for B and 1.23–1.42 s for C in the paired process. Those figures include JIT compilation and were excluded from steady-state results.

The independent oracle used exact binary rationals through the squared distance. It bracketed the mass equation, checked breakpoints and bisected, accepting a chord root after exact substitution. Exhaustive support enumeration and analytic formulas verified 226 oracle checks. Each candidate and the frozen baseline ran 185 common cases on each Julia/version optimization pair, for 740 records per pin. The suite measured both default and `-O0` and included direct and scaled calls, exact ties and near ties, endpoint levels, array and unsupported-call contracts, and range extremes. The 32-epsilon screen with an additive two-subnormal-spacing allowance was descriptive. The review included explicit stationarity and mixed-scale underflow checks, which reveal failures that an input-scale tolerance alone can hide.

## Independent review

- **Cancellation changes the stop decision.** With `H = 2^(precision(T)+3)`, `c = H`, and `v = [H, 1, 2^-20, 0, …]` at `n = 4096`, the exact threshold is `0.5` and the exact score is `0.7071067811871906` at zero and on the all-negative face. On Float32, A gave `0.00034526864`, B gave zero, and C gave `0.70710677`. Their absolute errors were `0.70676`, `0.70711`, and `1.21e-8`. Dividing by `‖v‖₂ + c` gives `2.63e-9`, `2.63e-9`, and `4.51e-17`. The large normalizer explains why the first two errors clear a scale-only check. In an exactly stationary face with `v = [H; fill(9,256)]` and `c = H + 2304`, A returned `6.986368` at default optimization and `111.78188` at `-O0` for Float32. C returned zero.
- **The all-subnormal A revision is narrower than the geometry.** It fixed the common case `v = 0`, `c = 2047δ`, and `n = 4096`, where the exact score is `31.984375δ` and `δ` is the smallest positive representable value. With the first gradient changed to `floatmin(T)` and the radius increased by the same amount, A and B returned zero. C returned the correctly rounded `32δ` for Float32 and Float64.
- **Generic API compatibility blocks C’s pin.** Its face gather calls the type constructor after the classifier uses `convert`. A BigFloat point stored at 256-bit precision and evaluated at 128-bit ambient precision throws “empty projection face” for a unique face with exact score 1. Its Float16 cold norm returns 16,384 on 30,000 entries of 200 with zero radius, where the existing recovery path and rounded reference return 34,656. Long Float16 accumulations also exposed errors in all three candidates. On each requested Julia version, C’s package KKT file recorded 3,722 passes, 40 zero-allocation failures, and 28 inference errors. The inference failure comes from capturing a reassigned exponent in a closure. A and B passed their targeted KKT tests.
- **Pass counts can grow with the face.** For B, two unit entries followed by factorial-spaced negative values on the face, with radius 2, give 6, 10, and 14 excess passes at Float64 lengths 8, 12, and 16. At 2,048-bit BigFloat, length 96 gives 94 passes. The exact construction drops one entry per step. The common input-read probe separately measured at one million Float32 coordinates 13, 7, and 2 full gradient-read equivalents for A, B, and C on the many-leave case. These read counts omit scratch-buffer work and may change specialization. The timing harness used ordinary arrays.

No candidate establishes an end-to-end bound. The frozen PR’s first-order expression served as a diagnostic only.

## Reproduce or integrate

1. Fetch the two published SHAs into a separate repository and import the experimental refs from the bundle. The measurement archive contains `REPRODUCE.md` with command lines for both Julia versions, both optimization modes, the oracle, the extended review cases, and the paired measurements.
2. For a maintainer-authorized follow-up, take the C optimality diff only with `git diff faf2a00 556aff6 -- src/functions/normlinf.jl`. Keep the corrected master kernel and the existing public dispatch. Use `convert(W, value)` consistently for classification and gather, remove the boxed scale capture, and restore the existing cold-norm precision. Add a same-type bounded-depth treatment for long low-precision sums or document the supported domain with maintainer agreement.
3. Re-run the independent and package suites with the cancellation, subnormal, BigFloat and Float16 regressions. Use the paired data to evaluate the ordinary-path performance cost before choosing whether to ship the sorting route. For a shared-kernel follow-up, the first priority is preserving the small compensated mass excess when the projected support changes.

The experimental refs remain local and disposable. PR #981 and master were untouched.
