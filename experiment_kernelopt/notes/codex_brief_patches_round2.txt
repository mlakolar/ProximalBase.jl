What changed since round 1, and what I ask you to verify for each:

A3 (replaces A and A2). `shrink` is exactly master's (the select-based `shrink` of patch A is gone: it was measured 2.4x slower than master on Julia 1.10 for 16-entry Float32 arrays, in three fresh processes, and 1.38x for value_and_prox! on a Broadwell node). Only `_shrink_nonneg(v, c) = (v - min(max(v, -c), c)) + 0` (two-operand selects) is added and used on the ordinary branch of `_l1_prox_threshold`. So the comparison that matters is A3 against MASTER, not against A. On Zen 3 and Broadwell cluster nodes the clamp form measured about 0.5x of master's shrink loop and the select-sum form of A about 0.83x; on the Raptor Lake workstation you measured the opposite order between the two candidates. Please (1) re-verify the equivalence claim for the actual package function and public routes; (2) look for any existing ordinary path that A3 makes slower than MASTER by more than 5% (scalar prox_block as a coordinate-descent step would call it, tiny arrays of 1 to 16 entries in Float32 and Float64, weighted and unweighted, in place, on Julia 1.13 and 1.10); (3) say whether the tests in the diff now exercise the helper and the public routes adequately; (4) give your view on the remaining design question: two functions (`shrink` unchanged, `_shrink_nonneg` on the ordinary branch) versus giving `shrink` itself the new formula for IEEE types (one implementation, but `shrink` changes at negative thresholds, outside its documented domain c >= 0).

V (replaces C and C2). The unweighted NormL1 value and the NormL2 value use one @simd loop (`_value_loop`, @noinline) only for strided Float32/Float64 arrays with 16 <= length <= 1024, and master's code otherwise. SqrNormL2 is no longer touched. Rationale for 1024: Base's pairwise reduction is itself a single @simd loop for at most 1024 entries (its block size), so the error class should not change; rationale for 16 and @noinline: your singleton finding. Please verify (1) the claim about Base's reduction for n <= 1024 on Julia 1.13 and 1.10 by reading Base (base/reduce.jl in the Julia install) and state precisely how the two loops differ (first elements, lane/unroll count) and what that implies for worst-case error; (2) that lengths below 16 and above 1024 run master's arithmetic bit for bit and with no measurable slowdown (the extra length comparison and code layout); (3) the first sizes inside the window (16, 17, 24, 31, 32, 33) on both Julia versions, since a call to an out-of-line loop has to beat Base there; (4) dispatch: which array types reach the new loop for each function.

D3 (replaces D). The guard is now `_l2_threshold_decides(c, n)`: `c >= n * 2^-485` (Float64), `c >= n * 2^-51` (Float32), false for other types; no square of c. Please verify the inequality argument for this stronger condition including the conversion of n to the float type, and re-run your zero-group timing cases (tiny normal threshold where the guard fails, ordinary thresholds, dense nonzero groups, sizes 1, 5, 1024, both core types if you can). I measured about +7 to +15% (roughly 0.3 ns per group) for zero groups of 1 to 5 entries when the threshold is below the guard (c < n * 2^-485, e.g. level floatmin), and gains or no change elsewhere. Say whether you consider a zero group at a threshold below 2^-485 per entry an "existing ordinary hot path" under the rule, and whether there is a cheaper formulation.

B2 (replaces B; a trade-off, not proposed). Blocks of 256 steps; the generic per-entry loop from the first block with a product out of range on; arrays shorter than 8 and zero or non-finite levels skip the block tests; no `invoke`. On the workstation I measured: ordinary step arrays of 64 or more entries 0.39–0.47x (Float64) and 0.14–0.30x (Float32) of master; fewer than 8 entries +10–25%; one exceptional step in arrays of 8 to 64 entries +12–80%; all steps zero +7–40%; on an efficiency core 8 ordinary Float64 steps +38%. Verify correctness of the blocked structure (aliasing y === x and y === γ across block boundaries, the tail loop being exactly the generic expression, validation order) and say whether you see a formulation that removes the failure-path cost; otherwise confirm that this is a genuine trade-off for the maintainer.
