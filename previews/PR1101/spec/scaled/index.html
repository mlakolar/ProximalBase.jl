Range clauses

See Scoring optimality for the call described below.

Domain. The call evaluates the existing score of the leaf on the lazy gradient v/α, with the rounding and the range limits of that evaluation, and then multiplies by α; underflow, overflow and NaN are not recovered. The value agrees with the score of the scaled function up to rounding when 1/α, the entries v[i]/α and the arithmetic of the leaf stay in the range of the working type. The limits are those of the leaf: for Scaled(SqrNormL2(2.0^1000), 2.0^-1000) at v = [0.0], x = [2.0^600] the quotients are representable but the gradient entry of the leaf overflows, and the call returns +Inf where the score of the scaled function is 2.0^600. Outside the range of v/α the value is the one the evaluation gives, which need not be an infinity or NaN: Scaled(SqrNormL2(1.0), 1e-300) at v = [1e10], x = [1.0] gives +Inf (the score of the scaled function is 1e10), and Scaled(ZeroFunction(), 2.0) at v = [nextfloat(0.0)] gives 0.0 (the quotient underflows).

The reciprocal of the factor takes part in the type promotion. An ordinary Int factor, as in 2 * f, introduces Float64: data of type Float16 or Float32 then widen to Float64, while BigFloat data remain BigFloat.

@assert kkt_violation(Scaled(SqrNormL2(1.0), 1e-300), [1e10], [1.0], 1) == Inf
@assert kkt_violation(Scaled(SqrNormL2(1.0), 1e-30), [1e10], [1.0], 1) ≈ 1e10
@assert kkt_violation(Scaled(SqrNormL2(2.0^1000), 2.0^-1000), [0.0], [2.0^600], 1) == Inf
@assert kkt_violation(Scaled(ZeroFunction(), 2.0), [nextfloat(0.0)], [0.0], 1) == 0.0
@assert kkt_violation(2 * NormL1(0.5f0), Float32[1, 2], Float32[0, 3], 2) isa Float64
@assert kkt_violation(2 * NormL1(big"0.5"), [big"1.0", big"2.0"], [big"0.0", big"3.0"], 2) isa BigFloat

Nested positive finite scales retain their exponents when multiplying them would overflow or underflow. Built-in recovery paths can fold the total scale into the level of NormL1 or NormL1Nonneg or into both levels of ElasticNet, or recover a representable squared-norm value. When the fold takes the ridge level of an ElasticNet below floatmin while folding a step, that level is dropped, since its ridge factor 1/(1 + γλ2) rounds to one. An infinite level of NormL1, NormL1Nonneg or ElasticNet stays a hard constraint when the factor and the step leave the range. The folds of an ElasticNet keep an infinite level as it is and fold the other level as they fold it beside a finite level; a fold that overflows is not used. With λ1 = Inf, a folded ridge level acts on the entries with a zero weight; with λ2 = Inf and a nonnegative step, the proximal point is 0 at every entry, whatever λ1. The scaled value of an ElasticNet is recovered as for any function with levels. For a finite positive factor, where that gives no finite value (for example ElasticNet(2.0^-1000, 2.0^1000) under the factor 2.0^-600 at [2.0^300], whose levels do not fold by one factor), or 0 at a point where the function is not exactly 0, the scaled values of the ℓ₁ part and of the ridge part, each recovered as for NormL1 and SqrNormL2 and rounded on its own, are added; the sum replaces that result if it is finite after conversion to its type, and the first result is kept otherwise. This is not a promise that arbitrary user-defined functions acquire numerical range recovery. GroupNormLinf has no fold of its level for the proximal operator: under Scaled its proximal operator receives the step αγ as the wrapper rounds it, and an αγ that overflows reaches it as Inf. A group that reaches the infinite-step exit gives +0; a group with a zero weight, or a zero level with a finite weight, keeps the identity, because those exits come first (see the group ℓ∞ norm).

Endpoint clauses

Scaled(f,0) simplifies only when a domain-preserving representation is known. For finite-data built-in losses, quadratics, centered squared norms and affine functions it returns ZeroFunction(). A zero-scaled finite-level NormL1 with finite weights also returns ZeroFunction. Infinite weights are retained in NormL1(0,w) so their fixed-zero restrictions survive. A zero-scaled ElasticNet with finite levels likewise returns ZeroFunction for finite weights and ElasticNet(0,0,w) for infinite ones. A zero-scaled finite-level NormL1Nonneg is NormL1Nonneg(0,w) for any weights, which keeps its domain x ≥ 0 and the restrictions of infinite weights. With finite weights it is the indicator of the orthant, whose proximal operator is the projection max(v, 0), not the identity; an infinite weight keeps its fixed zero (0 * NormL1Nonneg(1.0, [Inf]) maps a finite input to +0). Infinite NormL1, NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet levels and non-finite parameters of other function families can make construction throw ArgumentError. A custom function's zero scale has no generic supported representation and throws. The indicator IndNonnegative does not depend on its scale: Scaled(IndNonnegative(), 0) and 0 * IndNonnegative() return the indicator itself. Inspect the constructed object rather than assume it is always a Scaled.

using ProximalBase
@assert 0 * SqrNormL2(2.0) isa ZeroFunction
h = 0 * NormL1(1.0,[Inf,1.0])
@assert h isa NormL1
@assert prox(h,[2.0,3.0]) == [0,3]
prox(h,[2.0,3.0])
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 3.0
z = 0 * NormL1Nonneg(2.0)
@assert z isa NormL1Nonneg && z.λ0 == 0
@assert z([1.0, 0.0]) == 0 && z([1.0, -1.0]) == Inf
@assert prox(z,[-2.0,3.0]) == [0,3]

Limitations and non-guarantees

  • Nested wrappers whose level does not fold. For two positive finite factors, the constructor merges the wrappers when the product is a normal number and keeps them nested otherwise; zero and infinite factors follow the endpoint rules of the constructor. For a chain around NormL1, NormL1Nonneg, ElasticNet, GroupNormL2, GroupNormLinf, NormL2 or NormLinf the product of the factors is folded into the level (both levels of ElasticNet, an infinite one being kept as it is) when the result is a nonzero finite number that the type of the level accepts, or the level is zero: a normal number, or a subnormal one when the folded level is a Float32 or a Float64 (a Float16 level folds only to a normal number). The chain then has the score of the function with that level, in each form that the function has (NormLinf has the full-point form and no form for an entry or a group; GroupNormLinf has the margin at a zero group, the distance at a nonzero one and the full-point form). A subnormal folded level has fewer significant bits than a normal one, and the coefficient of an entry is that rounded level times the weight. Where it does not fold, the three-argument screen of NormL1, NormL1Nonneg and ElasticNet, and the four-argument form of NormL1, NormL1Nonneg, ElasticNet, GroupNormL2 and GroupNormLinf at a zero coordinate or group, are computed from the coefficient $a\,\lambda\,w$ of the entry or group, with $a$ the product of the factors, $\lambda$ the level and $w$ the weight. For a positive finite $\lambda$ and $w$ the significands of the three are multiplied and their exponents are applied in one last step, without forming $a\lambda$ or dividing v by $a$. A zero weight gives the coefficient 0 (also under an infinite level), and an infinite weight or level gives Inf. For finite entries of v the value is $\lvert v_i\rvert-a\lambda w_i$ for a coordinate ($-v_i-a\lambda w_i$ for NormL1Nonneg), $\lVert v_G\rVert_2-a\lambda w_G$ for a group of GroupNormL2 and $\lVert v_G\rVert_1-a\lambda w_G$ for a group of GroupNormLinf, up to the rounding of the coefficient, of the sum over the group ($\lVert v_G\rVert_1$, or $\lVert v_G\rVert_2$ for GroupNormL2) and of the difference in the type of the call. A NaN entry gives NaN. An infinite entry gives what the three-argument screen gives for NormL1 and ElasticNet (Inf - Inf is NaN), and Inf for GroupNormL2 and GroupNormLinf. An ElasticNet with an infinite λ2 gives -Inf at a zero coordinate, whatever v holds. Scaled(Scaled(NormL1(1.0), 2.0^-600), 2.0^-600) at v = [0.25] returns 0.25, the coefficient $2^{-1200}$ rounding to zero in Float64. The four-argument form at a nonzero coordinate or group (an entry in no group of a GroupNormL2 or GroupNormLinf counts as nonzero when x is nonzero there, and a zero entry of a nonzero group selects that group, which is nonzero) and the full-point form throw an ArgumentError for such a chain, and the three-argument and four-argument forms of NormL2 and NormLinf throw an ArgumentError, also at a zero coordinate and under a chain that does not fold.
  • An infinite factor. Scaled(f, Inf) and Inf * f give one wrapper with an infinite factor, also when f is itself scaled, because the constructor merges the factors. kkt_violation(h, v, x) and the four-argument form at a nonzero coordinate or group throw an ArgumentError for it (an entry in no group of a GroupNormL2 or GroupNormLinf counts as nonzero when x is nonzero there). For a finite supplied v, the lazy gradient v/α is zero and a zero point has these values: -Inf for a penalized coordinate with a positive coefficient, and NaN (Inf * 0) where the coefficient is zero and for an entry in no group of a GroupNormL2 or GroupNormLinf; for smooth functions with a local form it is α times the score at v = 0.
  • A stack that nests an infinite factor with another Scaled wrapper. Scaled(f, α) and α * f do not produce it, because they merge the infinite factor; a direct call of the parameterized constructor Scaled{F,T}(f, α) does. For such a stack the three-argument screen and the four-argument value at a zero coordinate or group are unspecified: they can differ from version 0.5, and no particular value, NaN included, is promised. The four-argument form at a nonzero coordinate or group and the full-point form throw an ArgumentError.
  • Chains around LinearFunction and SqrNormL2. The product of the factors is applied to the gradient entry of the function, as the gradient of the chain applies it, and the score is $\lvert v_i+a\,d_i\rvert$ in all three forms (an uncentered SqrNormL2 at a zero coordinate, and ZeroFunction, have $\lvert v_i\rvert$). The three-argument screen of such a chain around LinearFunction and a centered SqrNormL2 was incorrect before 0.6: it returned the screen of the function at the unscaled v.
  • Least squares and quadratics. The full-point form uses the leaf's smooth stationarity score. Infinite factors and retained chains whose factors do not fold throw ArgumentError. The coordinate and group forms throw ArgumentError, including at zero.
  • Functions without the forms. SqrtLeastSquaresLoss and IndNonnegative have no four-argument or full-point score under Scaled; the call throws a MethodError (the call names the Scaled function). For a positive finite or infinite factor, Scaled(f, α) forwards the supported three-argument screen of an indicator f with the supplied gradient and returns its result unscaled. For IndNonnegative the result is -v[i]. NormLinf has the full-point form and, as NormL2 does, throws an ArgumentError for its other forms, also under Scaled. A function defined outside the package is treated as in Writing your own function.
lin = Scaled(Scaled(LinearFunction([1.0]), 1e-200), 1e-200)    # the product of the factors underflows Float64
@assert lin.f isa Scaled
@assert kkt_violation(lin, [0.25], 1) == 0.25 && kkt_violation(lin, [0.25], [0.7]) == 0.25
nl = Scaled(Scaled(NormL1(1.0), 2.0^-600), 2.0^-600)
@assert kkt_violation(nl, [0.25], 1) == 0.25                # the coefficient 2^-1200 is not representable; the exponent is applied last
gl = Scaled(Scaled(GroupNormL2(1.0, [[1, 2]], [1.0]), 2.0^-600), 2.0^-600)
@assert kkt_violation(gl, [0.3, 0.4], [0.0, 0.0], 1) == 0.5   # a zero group: the norm of v, less the coefficient
refused(f) = try f(); false catch e; e isa ArgumentError end
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(nl, [0.25], [1.0], 1)) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(nl, [0.25], [1.0]))
inf = Scaled(NormL1(1.0), Inf)
@assert kkt_violation(inf, [3.0], [0.0], 1) == -Inf
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(inf, [3.0], [1.0], 1)) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(inf, [3.0], [0.0]))
t = 2.0^-600
@assert !(Scaled(Scaled(LinearFunction([1.0]), 2.0), Inf).f isa Scaled)             # the constructor merges the infinite factor
@assert !(Scaled(Scaled(Scaled(NormL1(1.0), t), t), Inf).f isa Scaled)
@assert !((Inf * (t * (t * NormL1(1.0)))).f isa Scaled)
raw(f, a) = Scaled{typeof(f), typeof(a)}(f, a)
handbuilt = raw(Scaled(NormL1(1.0), t), Inf)                    # two wrappers, one of them infinite: only a direct call builds it
@assert handbuilt.f isa Scaled
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(handbuilt, [0.25], [1.0], 1)) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(handbuilt, [0.25], [0.0]))
@assert isnan(kkt_violation(Scaled(NormL1(0.0), Inf), [3.0], [0.0], 1))
nlinf = Scaled(Scaled(NormLinf(2.0^600), 2.0^-600), 2.0^-600)    # the product 2^-1200 is not a normal number; the level 2^-600 is
@assert nlinf.f isa Scaled
@assert kkt_violation(nlinf, [0.25, -0.5], [0.0, 0.0]) == kkt_violation(NormLinf(2.0^-600), [0.25, -0.5], [0.0, 0.0])
nflinf = Scaled(Scaled(NormLinf(1.0), 2.0^-600), 2.0^-600)             # the level 2^-1200 does not fold
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(nflinf, [0.25], [1.0])) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(nflinf, [0.25], [0.0]))
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(nflinf, [0.25], [1.0], 1)) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(nflinf, [0.25], 1))
@assert refused(() -> kkt_violation(h2, [3.0, 1.0], [0.0, 0.0], 1)) && refused(() -> kkt_violation(h2, [3.0, 1.0], 1))
sqrtls = SqrtLeastSquaresLoss([1.0, 2.0], [1.0 0.0; 0.0 1.0])
nomethod = try kkt_violation(Scaled(sqrtls, 2.0), [1.0, 2.0], [0.0, 1.0], 1); false catch e; e isa MethodError end
@assert nomethod
