GroupSCAD numerical contract

This page is the authored contract of GroupSCAD. Read How to read the numerical contracts for authority and ownership.

Identity

GroupSCAD is the group smoothly clipped absolute deviation penalty. See the implementation, the function guide and the API coverage matrix.

Definition and domain

For disjoint nonempty groups $G_k$ of linear indices,

\[f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_{\lambda_k,a}\bigl(\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2\bigr),\qquad p_{\lambda,a}(t)=\begin{cases}\lambda t, & 0\le t\le\lambda,\\ \lambda t-(t-\lambda)^2/(2(a-1)), & \lambda<t<a\lambda,\\ (a+1)\lambda^2/2, & t\ge a\lambda,\end{cases}\]

where $\lambda_k$ is the effective coefficient of group k: $\lambda_0w_k$ when λ0 and w_k are finite and positive, and otherwise the endpoint table gives the group's map and term. The formula for $p$ is that of finite positive $\lambda$ and finite a, as for the scalar SCAD; at a = Inf and finite positive $\lambda$ it is $\lambda t$. The shape a is not rescaled by the weights. Entries in no group are unpenalized.

Zero weights. A group with w_k = 0 is unpenalized, whatever λ0 (Inf included): the proximal map leaves its entries unchanged (copied to the destination, see Storage below the endpoint table) and its term is zero. For the group's proximal map and term, this rule comes before the others on this page.

Parameter type. GroupSCAD(λ0, groups, w; a = 3.7) uses T = float(promote_type(typeof(λ0), eltype(w))) and converts a to T, so the default a = 3.7 does not promote a Float32 function to Float64. GroupSCAD(λ0, groups; a = 3.7) uses T = float(typeof(λ0)) and the weights $w_k=\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}$, computed in promote_type(T, Float64) and converted to T. This differs from the scalar SCAD, whose one-argument form promotes the level and the shape together. The level and the weights are nonnegative and not NaN; a is above 2 (Inf is admitted) and not NaN. a is checked before its conversion and again after it: a value above 2 whose conversion to T is not above 2 (a = 2.00000001 in Float32), and a finite value whose conversion to T is infinite (a = 1e39 in Float32), throw an ArgumentError. The two public constructors copy the weights, the list of groups and each group that is not a range, and keep their order; a range group can be kept as given (when every group is a range of one concrete type) and must not be mutated afterwards. With no groups the value is zero and the proximal map is the identity.

Traits

GroupSCAD is proximable, and its convexity, smoothness and separability traits are false. Instances with a positively weighted group of finite positive level and finite shape are nonconvex.

Supported arguments

The value, prox, prox! and value_and_prox! take one-based real arrays: Array (a matrix is indexed linearly by the groups), SparseIterate and BlockIterate, read and written by indexing their entries. The destination of prox! and value_and_prox! has a concrete floating-point element type; prox allocates its destination with the element type float(eltype(x)), which must pass the same check. The step is a nonnegative real scalar. prox_block!(out, f, u, k, γ) and the allocating prox_block(f, u, k, γ) take compact vectors in the order of groups[k] and do not change an iterate. BlockIterate(p, f), BlockIterate(T, p, f) and BlockIterate(x, f) take the groups of f, which must then partition the entries, with the copying rule of the constructors above.

The numerical target is homogeneous CPU Float32 and Float64 input, destination, parameters and step. Some other type combinations dispatch, without a precision guarantee.

Preconditions

The typed constructor GroupSCAD{T, G}(λ0, a, groups, w) validates its arguments and stores groups and w without copying them: do not mutate the groups or the weights passed to it afterwards.

Exact in-place use (y === x, and out === u for compact blocks) is supported. Other overlaps that Base.mightalias detects are rejected; an overlap that it does not detect is unsupported. For prox_block!, out must be independent of iterate storage, as the pure block contract states. A GroupSCAD has no buffer, and its oracles do not write to the object.

Results

prox! returns the destination, prox_block! returns out, and value_and_prox! returns the value at the stored output: it equals f(y) up to the order in which sums of squares are accumulated.

Ordinary-path clauses

Exact-arithmetic target. For positive finite λ0, w_k and γ, a finite a and a group with finite nonzero norm $r=\lVert u_{G_k}\rVert_2$, the target replaces the group by $(t_*/r)\,u_{G_k}$, where $t_*$ is a global minimizer of $p_{\lambda_k,a}(t)+(t-r)^2/(2\gamma)$ over $t\ge0$, the one that the scalar SCAD proximal rule selects at the input r: the group is unchanged when $t_*=r$, +0 when $t_*=0$, and otherwise scaled by $t_*/r$, which is below one. At a tie between minimizers the smaller magnitude is taken, so a tie with zero gives +0. The middle piece has positive, zero or negative curvature as γ passes through a - 1; the scalar contract owns the comparisons. Groups with nonfinite entries follow the endpoint table.

Computed result. When the input, the destination, the parameters and the step are Float32 or Float64, the decisions use floating-point arithmetic on the computed norm and the level as formed, and can round near a cutoff: for a = 3, γ = 5, $\lambda_k=1$ and r = sqrt(20.0) the result is +0. The stored entries $q\,u_i$ are rounded, so the stored direction can change: for $\lambda_k=1$, γ = 1 and [1.5, nextfloat(0.0)] the result is [0.5, 0.0]. $t_*$ can round to r inside the middle piece, as for the input [1.2, 1.6] with λ_k = 1, γ = 1e-20 and a = 3.7: the group is then unchanged, and its value is $p_{\lambda_k,a}$ at r, below the plateau.

The value of such a group is $p_{\lambda_k,a}$ at its computed norm, up to rounding.

At a = Inf each group follows the rules of GroupNormL2 at the coefficient $\lambda_k$: soft group shrinkage and, for finite positive stored λ0 and w_k, the value $\lambda_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$; the endpoint table gives the other cases (a hard group at a zero input has the value zero).

Range clauses

For positive finite stored λ0 and w_k, the decisions above do not require the products $\lambda_0w_k$, $\gamma\lambda_k$ or $a\lambda_k$ to be representable: an overflowing finite product is not treated as a stored infinity, and an underflowing one is not treated as a stored zero. A scaled norm is the recovery route, when needed, for a group whose sum of squares overflows or underflows the input type. For a finite shape and a finite positive step, that route writes $r=m_r2^{e_r}$ and $\lambda_k=m_\lambda2^{e_\lambda}$ (significands in $[1/2,1)$): for $r\le\lambda_k$, decided on these parts, the group is soft thresholded; when $e_r-e_\lambda$ is above exponent(floatmax(R)) + 1 for the recovery type R the group is unchanged; otherwise the scalar kernel decides the problem scaled by $2^{-e_\lambda}$. For a = 3.7, γ = 1.0, $\lambda_k=1/2$ and r = floatmax(Float64), $e_r-e_\lambda=1024$, which is not above that bound, although $r/\lambda_k$ exceeds floatmax(Float64); the group is unchanged. The stored result is limited by the range of the destination type. Float64 examples, each with its own parameters:

using ProximalBase
# a = 3.7, λ = 2^1000; the level in units of r = 2^-30 (2^1030) overflows. γλ = 2^-60 < r ≤ λ: soft thresholding
g = GroupSCAD(2.0^1000, [1:1], [1.0])
@assert prox_block(g, [2.0^-30], 1, 2.0^-1060) == [2.0^-30 - 2.0^-60]
# λ0 = w = 1e-200: λ0*w rounds to 0, and r/λ ≈ 2.2e90. At a = 3.7 (nonconvex) the group is kept, since r/λ > √(γ(a+1))
u = [1e-310, 2e-310]
k = GroupSCAD(1e-200, [1:2], [1e-200])
@assert prox_block(k, u, 1, 1e100) == u
# at a = 1e200 (convex middle piece) r < γλ: zero
k2 = GroupSCAD(1e-200, [1:2], [1e-200]; a = 1e200)
@assert prox_block(k2, u, 1, 1e100) == [0.0, 0.0]
# the value on the middle piece with a near floatmax: p(0.9e308) ≈ 4.95e307
h = GroupSCAD(1.0, [1:1], [1.0]; a = 1e308)
@assert isapprox(h([0.9e308]), 4.95e307; rtol = 1e-14)

When the ordinary sum of the group terms is at or above floatmax(T)/2 or, for Float32 and Float64, small enough that terms rounded into the subnormal range may have lost accuracy, the value is recomputed from scaled terms in at least Float64, unless the endpoint table already makes the value NaN or Inf. Within a few units in the last place of the overflow threshold, a value can be floatmax(T) or Inf.

Endpoint clauses

The rows apply per group in this order and are selected by the stored parameters (iszero and isinf of λ0, w_k, a and γ), not by whether their product overflows or underflows. Other groups and the entries in no group are independent. Steps are nonnegative and not NaN.

Storage. "Unchanged", "Identity" and "signed zeros kept" mean that the entries, and the entries in no group, are copied to the destination under the storage rule of the iterates and the conversion to its element type. A dense destination of the same element type keeps a -0.0; a zero written to an absent coordinate of a SparseIterate leaves it absent, and it reads +0. A route that sets a group to zero writes +0.

Failure behavior

The constructors throw an ArgumentError for a negative or NaN level or weight, a shape that is NaN or not above 2, a shape whose conversion to T is not above 2 or turns a finite value into Inf, an empty group, a nonpositive index, an index in two groups and offset axes, and a DimensionMismatch for a weight count that differs from the group count. The public constructors check the level, the weights and the shape before conversion and also throw an ArgumentError for complex weights. The array oracles and prox_block! throw before any write: an ArgumentError for a negative or NaN step (also when a group of the function is fixed at +0 by its parameters), entries that are not real, a destination whose element type is not a concrete floating-point type (an integer or an abstract type such as AbstractFloat), a detected partial overlap and offset axes; a DimensionMismatch for different shapes, groups beyond the argument and a compact length that differs from the group; a BoundsError for a group number out of range or a Bool. A SymmetricSparseIterate argument or output of a proximal oracle with a scalar step throws an ArgumentError before any write.

At a real array point (Array, SparseIterate or BlockIterate), kkt_violation(f, v, x, k) returns at a zero group the signed screen of GroupNormL2 with the level and the weight as separate factors: $\lVert v_{G_k}\rVert_2-\lambda_0w_k$ for finite converted gradient entries and finite λ0 and w_k, up to rounding; $\lVert v_{G_k}\rVert_2$ for a zero weight, whatever λ0; otherwise the rules of that screen for NaN and infinite values. At an entry in no group it returns abs(v[k]). It throws an ArgumentError at a nonzero group, as do kkt_violation(f, v, x) and kkt_violation(f, v, i). Layout, count and extent errors are those of GroupNormL2. A call with a SymmetricSparseIterate point that passes the shared argument checks throws a MethodError.

Working type of the zero-group screen

The zero-group screen checks finiteness after converting the gradient entries to W = float(promote_type(eltype(v), eltype(x))). A finite stored entry that overflows on conversion is treated as infinite.

Limitations and non-guarantees

An instance with a positively weighted group of finite positive level and finite shape is nonconvex (see Traits). The target of the proximal map is a global minimizer of the radial problem in exact arithmetic (see the ordinary-path clauses for the computed result), and GroupSCAD carries no statement about the global minimization of an objective that contains it. At a zero group the score is a rounded signed screen of the local condition $\lVert v_{G_k}\rVert_2\le\lambda_k$ (for finite positive factors and finite converted gradient entries); a nonpositive score is not a proof of that condition and does not imply a global minimizer. The score at a nonzero group and the full-point form are not implemented.

Scaled(GroupSCAD(...), α) multiplies the value by α and calls the proximal operator with the effective step $\alpha\gamma$ as rounded, as for the scalar SCAD. The effective step is validated: a negative or NaN one (Scaled(f, Inf) at γ = 0 gives NaN) throws an ArgumentError before any write, and the ordered endpoint table applies to a valid effective step. A zero α throws an ArgumentError. There is no compact block method and no point-aware score under Scaled (MethodError).

g = GroupSCAD(2.0^-700, [1:2], [1.0])
@assert prox(Scaled(g, 2.0^600), [0.6, 0.8], 2.0^600) == [0.0, 0.0]     # αγ = 2^1200 rounds to Inf
@assert prox_block(g, [0.6, 0.8], 1, 2.0^1023) == [0.6, 0.8]            # a large finite step keeps the group

There is no restrict and no array (per-entry or per-group) step: calls that pass the shared argument checks throw a MethodError, except where a route shared with other functions rejects a SymmetricSparseIterate destination, or the allocating prox(f, S, steps), with an ArgumentError. The scalar-step refusal of a SymmetricSparseIterate is under Failure behavior. A SparseIterate destination is written entry by entry, as for GroupNormL2.

For a finite shape, a Float32 input whose norm exceeds floatmax(Float32), with a Float64 destination, is covered by the tests of #534. Other mixed type combinations carry no precision guarantee; the numerical targets are the homogeneous ones in Supported arguments.

History

The restoration issue and its pull request record the decisions: the endpoint table, the parameter-type rule, the refusal of the nonzero-group score, the rounded effective step under Scaled, the refusal of non-floating destinations and the uncopied buffers of the typed constructor, as for GroupMCP. The archived legacy/full implementation formed the group norm in the input type and could return NaN for representable outputs (#534); GroupSCAD forms the scaled norm in the recovery type and has finite-shape tests for the reproducers. The infinite-weight convention follows #250.
