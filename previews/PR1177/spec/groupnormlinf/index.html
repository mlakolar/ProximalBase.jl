GroupNormLinf numerical contract

This page gathers the existing numerical rules for GroupNormLinf. The text is preserved from the earlier manual. Clause conversion and status assignments are a separate review step; unstated status remains unclassified. Read How to read the numerical contracts for authority and ownership.

Preconditions

A GroupNormLinf object keeps a buffer for the threshold, with the length of its longest group, which the first group that needs one allocates. The buffer is left untouched by the parameter exits, by NaN and infinite groups, by a zero fill decided before the threshold (the computed radius reaches the computed sum of magnitudes; a threshold whose level is zero has already used the buffer) and, in prox_block!, when out is an Array with a floating-point element type, u is an Array or a SubArray of an Array, and !Base.mightalias(out, u) (so not when out === u): out is then the buffer. The same object must not be used from several tasks at once while its buffer may be in use (Scaled wrappers of one object share it, and so do the groups of a threaded loop of prox_block!(u, g, u, k, γ)), and it must not be used reentrantly, also within one task: a nested call on the same object, from a getindex of an argument while the threshold runs, can overwrite the candidates of the running threshold if it reaches the threshold with the shared buffer. Construct a fresh GroupNormLinf for each task, or synchronize the calls (ownership).

Range clauses

The shared range guarantees describe the range check referenced here. The endpoint table and the ElasticNet range clauses supply the endpoint and Julia 1.10 scaling context.

GroupNormLinf values apply the same check to their group terms λ0*w[k]*max|x[G_k]|, each formed in floating point as (λ0*w[k])*m with two rounded products when λ0*w[k] is a normal number (and by the three-factor kernel otherwise) and then converted to the output type, and may recompute from the exponents of w[k]*max|x[G_k]| with λ0 applied once.

For GroupNormLinf, let T be the common type of the input, output, level, weights and step (Float32 or Float64). For finite data and positive finite parameters in round-to-nearest arithmetic with gradual underflow, each stored entry is within 8 n eps(T) M + (2n + 1)δ of the exact proximal entry for r = γλ0w[k]. Here n ≥ 1 is the size of the group, M its largest magnitude and δ = nextfloat(zero(T)). The bound assumes n eps(T) ≤ 1/2, which the code does not check. This applies to the direct array and compact block calls. The parameter precedence for zero and infinite factors is given in the table below.

The exact nonnegative level at radius r is the maximum of zero and the levels (sum(abs(x[i]) for i in A) - r) / length(A) over nonempty subsets A of the group. Thus it changes by at most the largest entry perturbation and the absolute radius perturbation. A relative perturbation of the radius by at most η changes the level by at most η M / (1 - η): at the smaller radius a subset with a positive level has radius per entry at most M. The radius uses two products in the working type, or two products of significands followed by exponent scaling if the normal-range products cannot be used. With u = eps(T)/2, the two products contribute at most 4u M to the level error and subnormal radius rounding at most δ, including the Julia 1.10 ldexp allowance above.

If the computed radius is below the computed sum, the shared threshold contributes at most 4.5 n u M + 2nδ before accounting for the radius error. If the computed radius reaches the computed sum, that sum is at least (1 - (n - 1)u) times the exact sum. The subset formula then bounds the level for the computed radius by (n - 1)u M. If the rounded radius overflows in these units, the exact radius exceeds floatmax(T)/(1 + u)^2. The exact sum is at most 2 floatmax(T)/3 when its computed value is at most floatmax(T)/2, so zero is then the exact result.

If the first sum exceeds F/2, where F = floatmax(T), the group is read in units of 2^-k with k = ceil(log2(n)) + 2. Under the size hypothesis the rounded scaled sum is at most (F/4 + nδ)/(1 - n u) < F/2, so the first scaling suffices. The scaled entries incur at most δ of perturbation in those units. Applying the unscaled bound there and multiplying back gives (4.5n + 4)u M plus at most 2^k [(2n + 2) + (4.5n + 4)u]δ. Since 2^k < 8n, the latter term is at most 48 n^2 δ. The scaling trigger requires M > F/(3n), and 144 n^2 δ ≤ u F for Float32 and Float64 under the size hypothesis, so this term is at most n u M. Scaling the level back is exact where finite. Capping it at M, including when it overflows, can only decrease the error. Clipping selects an entry or the signed level, both already in T, so same-type storage adds no rounding error. With a different input or output type, entries are read in the floating type of the destination and the clipped values are converted to its element type on write.

The level comes from the same threshold as NormLinf, applied to the entries of one group, read through its indices without a copy. With finite data and positive finite parameters of the same type Float32 or Float64, and input and output of that type, the absolute error in each stored entry is at most $8n\varepsilon\max_{i\in G_k}\lvert x_i\rvert+(2n+1)\delta$ under the assumptions in the numerical contract. Here $n$ is the group size, $\varepsilon$ is eps(T) and $\delta$ is the smallest positive subnormal of T. The radius is formed as $(\gamma\lambda_0)w_k$ in floating point and then converted to the output type. When $\gamma\lambda_0$ is not a normal number, or the product leaves the normal range of the output type, it is formed from the significands and exponents of the three factors, so that an intermediate overflow or underflow does not decide it. The accuracy bound includes the case where the computed radius reaches the computed sum and the entire group becomes +0.

The value of a group is $\lambda_0w_k\max\lvert x_{G_k}\rvert$, formed in floating point as $(\lambda_0w_k)m$ with two rounded products when $\lambda_0w_k$ is a normal number and by the three-factor kernel in power-of-two units otherwise, and then converted to the output type; the sum over the groups may be recomputed in power-of-two units, accumulated in at least Float64, where it may have lost accuracy (a sum below K*floatmin(T) for K groups, whose terms may have rounded into the subnormal range) or class (at or above floatmax(T)/2), as for GroupNormL2 (value range limits). A sum of magnitudes of one group above floatmax(T)/2 is processed in power-of-two units. With a positive finite level and weight, prox! computes the clip of a group with finite entries whose sum overflows from the entries scaled by a power of two:

M = floatmax(Float64)
huge = GroupNormLinf(M, [1:2], [2.0])        # the radius γλ₀w = M/2 at γ = 0.25 is finite; λ₀w alone overflows
yh = prox(huge, [M, M], 0.25)
@assert yh[1] == yh[2] && isapprox(yh[1], 0.75M; rtol = 1e-14)
tiny = GroupNormLinf(2.0^-100, [1:2], [2.0^1000])   # γλ₀ underflows, γλ₀w = 2^-100 equals ‖x‖₁
@assert prox(tiny, [2.0^-100, 0.0], 2.0^-1000) == [0.0, 0.0]

Limitations and non-guarantees

The group optimality scores explain the scaling rules referenced here.

GroupNormLinf has no SymmetricSparseIterate, restrict, per-group step or compact block method under Scaled. Scaled(GroupNormLinf(...), α) multiplies the value by α and computes the array proximal operator with the step αγ; it has no folded level for the proximal operator, so a step αγ that overflows reaches the function as Inf (the scores of kkt_violation fold the level of a chain, as above). A group that reaches the infinite-step exit then gives +0, where the exact radius $\alpha\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ can be below the ℓ₁ norm of the group; a group with a zero weight or a zero level with a finite weight keeps the identity, because those exits come first.

Zero factor in Scaled

A zero α throws an ArgumentError.

Scaled step example

scaled = Scaled(GroupNormLinf(2.0^-1020, [1:2]), 2.0^1000)
@assert prox(scaled, [2000.0, 500.0], 2.0^30) == [0.0, 0.0]           # the step 2^1030 overflows
@assert prox(GroupNormLinf(2.0^-20, [1:2]), [2000.0, 500.0], 2.0^30) == [976.0, 500.0]   # the radius 2^10 = 1024 keeps the group
prox(scaled, [2000.0, 500.0], 2.0^30)
