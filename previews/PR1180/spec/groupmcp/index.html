GroupMCP numerical contract

This page is the authored contract of GroupMCP. Read How to read the numerical contracts for authority and ownership.

Identity

GroupMCP is the group minimax concave penalty. See the implementation, the function guide and the API coverage matrix.

Definition and domain

For disjoint nonempty groups $G_k$ of linear indices,

\[f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_{\lambda_k,a}\bigl(\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2\bigr),\qquad p_{\lambda,a}(t)=\begin{cases}\lambda t-t^2/(2a), & 0\le t\le a\lambda,\\ a\lambda^2/2, & t>a\lambda,\end{cases}\]

where $\lambda_k$ is the effective coefficient of group k: $\lambda_0w_k$ when λ0 and w_k are finite and positive, and otherwise the endpoint table gives the group's map and term. The formula for $p$ is that of finite positive $\lambda$ and a; at a = Inf and finite positive $\lambda$ it is $\lambda t$. The shape a is not rescaled by the weights. Entries in no group are unpenalized.

Zero weights. A group with w_k = 0 is unpenalized, whatever λ0 (Inf included): the proximal map leaves its entries unchanged and its term is zero. For the group's proximal map and term, this rule comes before the others on this page.

GroupMCP(λ0, groups, w; a = 3) converts λ0, w and a to T = float(promote_type(typeof(λ0), eltype(w), typeof(a))). GroupMCP(λ0, groups; a = 3) uses T = float(promote_type(typeof(λ0), typeof(a))) and the weights $w_k=\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}$, computed in promote_type(T, Float64) and converted to T. The level and the weights are nonnegative and not NaN; a is positive and not NaN. These two constructors copy the weights, the list of groups and each group that is not a range, and keep their order; a range group can be kept as given (when every group is a range of one concrete type) and must not be mutated afterwards. With no groups the value is zero and the proximal map is the identity.

Traits

GroupMCP is proximable, and its convexity, smoothness and separability traits are false. Instances with a positively weighted group of finite positive level and finite shape are generally nonconvex.

Supported arguments

The value, prox, prox! and value_and_prox! take one-based real arrays: Array (a matrix is indexed linearly by the groups), SparseIterate and BlockIterate, read and written by indexing their entries. The destination of prox! and value_and_prox! has a floating-point element type; prox allocates one. The step is a nonnegative real scalar. prox_block!(out, f, u, k, γ) and the allocating prox_block(f, u, k, γ) take compact vectors in the order of groups[k] and do not change an iterate. BlockIterate(p, f), BlockIterate(T, p, f) and BlockIterate(x, f) take the groups of f, which must then partition the entries, with the copying rule of the constructors above.

The numerical target is homogeneous CPU Float32 and Float64 input, destination, parameters and step. Some other type combinations dispatch, without a precision guarantee.

Preconditions

The typed constructor GroupMCP{T, G}(λ0, a, groups, w) validates its arguments and stores groups and w without copying them: do not mutate the groups or the weights passed to it afterwards.

Exact in-place use (y === x, and out === u for compact blocks) is supported. Other overlaps that Base.mightalias detects are rejected; an overlap that it does not detect is unsupported. For prox_block!, out must be independent of iterate storage, as the pure block contract states. A GroupMCP has no buffer, and its oracles do not write to the object.

Results

prox! returns the destination, prox_block! returns out, and value_and_prox! returns the value at the stored output: it equals f(y) up to the order in which sums of squares are accumulated.

Ordinary-path clauses

For positive finite λ0, w_k and γ, a finite a and a finite nonzero group, let r be the computed norm of the group and $\lambda_k$ the level as formed. The group is replaced by a minimizer $t_*$ of $p_{\lambda_k,a}(t)+(t-r)^2/(2\gamma)$ over $t\ge0$, along the group: for $\gamma<a$,

  • +0 when $r\le\gamma\lambda_k$;
  • unchanged when $r\ge a\lambda_k$;
  • otherwise scaled by $(r-\gamma\lambda_k)\,a/((a-\gamma)\,r)$, at most one;

and for $\gamma\ge a$, unchanged when $r^2>a\gamma\lambda_k^2$ and +0 otherwise. At a tie the result is +0. At $\gamma=a$ the hard rule applies, as for the scalar MCP.

When the input, the destination, the parameters and the step are Float32 or Float64, these comparisons are exact for the computed norm and the level as formed. They are not exact in the entries: the computed norm carries the rounding of the sum of squares, and a group within that rounding of a threshold can take either side.

The value of such a group is $p_{\lambda_k,a}$ at its computed norm, up to rounding.

At a = Inf each group follows the rules of GroupNormL2 at the coefficient $\lambda_k$: soft group shrinkage and the value $\lambda_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$.

Range clauses

For positive finite stored λ0 and w_k, the decisions above do not require the products $\lambda_0w_k$, $\gamma\lambda_k$, $a\lambda_k$ or $a\gamma\lambda_k^2$ to be representable: an overflowing finite product is not treated as a stored infinity, and an underflowing one is not treated as a stored zero. A scaled norm is the recovery route, when needed, for a group whose sum of squares overflows or underflows the input type. The stored result is limited by the range of the destination type. Three Float64 examples, each with its own parameters:

using ProximalBase
# a = 3, λ = 2^1000; the level in units of r = 2^-30 (2^1030) overflows. γλ = 2^-60 < r < aλ: firm
g = GroupMCP(2.0^1000, [1:1], [1.0])
@assert prox_block(g, [2.0^-30], 1, 2.0^-1060) == [2.0^-30 - 2.0^-60]
# a = γ = 1.5 * 2^1023, λ = 2^-993; aγ overflows. r² = 2^60 ≤ aγλ² = 2.25 * 2^60: zero
h = GroupMCP(2.0^-993, [1:1], [1.0]; a = 1.5 * 2.0^1023)
@assert prox_block(h, [2.0^30], 1, 1.5 * 2.0^1023) == [0.0]
# a = 1e200, λ0 = w = 1e-200; λ0*w rounds to 0. γλ ≈ 1e-300 > r: zero
u = GroupMCP(1e-200, [1:2], [1e-200]; a = 1e200)
@assert prox_block(u, [1e-310, 2e-310], 1, 1e100) == [0.0, 0.0]

When the ordinary sum of the group terms is at or above floatmax(T)/2 or, for Float32 and Float64, small enough that terms rounded into the subnormal range may have lost accuracy, the value is recomputed from scaled terms in at least Float64, unless the endpoint table already makes the value NaN or Inf. Within a few units in the last place of the overflow threshold, a value can be floatmax(T) or Inf.

Failure behavior

The constructors throw an ArgumentError for a negative or NaN level or weight, a nonpositive or NaN shape, an empty group, a nonpositive index, an index in two groups and offset axes, and a DimensionMismatch for a weight count that differs from the group count. The outer constructors check the level and the weights before conversion and also throw an ArgumentError for complex weights. The array oracles and prox_block! throw before any write: an ArgumentError for a negative or NaN step, entries that are not real, a destination whose element type is not floating point, a detected partial overlap and offset axes; a DimensionMismatch for different shapes, groups beyond the argument and a compact length that differs from the group; a BoundsError for a group number out of range or a Bool. A SymmetricSparseIterate argument or output of a proximal oracle with a scalar step throws an ArgumentError before any write.

At a real array point (Array, SparseIterate or BlockIterate), kkt_violation(f, v, x, k) returns at a zero group the signed screen of GroupNormL2 with the level and the weight as separate factors: $\lVert v_{G_k}\rVert_2-\lambda_0w_k$ for finite v, λ0 and w_k, up to rounding; $\lVert v_{G_k}\rVert_2$ for a zero weight, whatever λ0; otherwise the rules of that screen for NaN and infinite values. At an entry in no group it returns abs(v[k]). It throws an ArgumentError at a nonzero group, as do kkt_violation(f, v, x) and kkt_violation(f, v, i). Layout, count and extent errors are those of GroupNormL2. A call with a SymmetricSparseIterate point that passes the shared argument checks throws a MethodError.

Limitations and non-guarantees

The zero-group screen is a first-order score: a zero value does not imply a global minimizer. The score at a nonzero group and the full-point form are not implemented.

Scaled(GroupMCP(...), α) multiplies the value by α and calls the proximal operator with the effective step $\alpha\gamma$ as rounded, as for the scalar MCP; the rounded step then follows this page (a step that rounds to zero or Inf takes the zero-step or infinite-step row). A zero α throws an ArgumentError. There is no compact block method and no point-aware score under Scaled (MethodError).

g = GroupMCP(2.0^-700, [1:2], [1.0])
@assert prox(Scaled(g, 2.0^600), [0.6, 0.8], 2.0^600) == [0.0, 0.0]     # αγ = 2^1200 rounds to Inf
@assert prox_block(g, [0.6, 0.8], 1, 2.0^1023) == [0.6, 0.8]            # a large finite step keeps the group

There is no restrict and no array (per-entry or per-group) step: calls that pass the shared argument checks throw a MethodError, except where a route shared with other functions rejects a SymmetricSparseIterate destination, or the allocating prox(f, S, steps), with an ArgumentError. The scalar-step refusal of a SymmetricSparseIterate is under Failure behavior. A SparseIterate destination is written entry by entry, as for GroupNormL2.

Evidence

test/groupmcp.jl compares the proximal map with a BigFloat minimization of the radial objective that does not use the package: the scalar reference of test/mcp_reference.jl (candidate comparison at 256 bits, repeated at 4096 bits when two candidates are within its uncertainty, ties included) at the group norm computed in 512-bit BigFloat. It checks the objective at the computed output within a stated tolerance and, where the reference decision is stable under the rounding of the norm, the output itself. The cases tested are listed in the file and in test/TESTING.md.

History

The restoration issue and its pull request record the decisions: the endpoint table, the refusal of the nonzero-group score, the rounded effective step under Scaled, the refusal of non-floating destinations and the uncopied buffers of the typed constructor. The archived legacy/full implementation formed the group norm in the input type and could return NaN for representable outputs (#534). The infinite-weight convention follows #250.
