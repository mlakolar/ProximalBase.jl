Cardinality selection

NormL0 makes a keep-or-drop decision at each penalized entry. IndBallL0 projects with a shared budget of counted nonzeros. The NormL0 contract and the IndBallL0 contract own the exact comparison, input and endpoint rules.

Scalar keep-or-drop decision

For finite $v$ and positive finite level $\lambda$ and step $\gamma$, keeping a nonzero entry at $v$ costs $\gamma\lambda$ in the proximal objective. Dropping it to zero costs $v^2/2$. A strict cost advantage keeps $v$ when $v^2>2\gamma\lambda$. At equality the implementation chooses zero from the two minimizers.

With homogeneous Float32 inputs, the scalar comparison widens both products to Float64. On the homogeneous Float64 route, the kernel can use the ordinary products when the right product is normal and the rounded products differ. When that comparison cannot decide, the kernel compares normalized exponents and aligns integer significand products for the close cases. This preserves the tie decision for the represented inputs even when the ordinary products would overflow or underflow.

Projection with a support budget

Keeping an independent coordinate saves its squared magnitude in the squared-distance objective. Under a budget $s$, a minimum keeps the largest magnitudes up to that budget and copies the chosen values unchanged. For $x=(3,-2,1)$, a budget of two gives $(3,-2,0)$. At unit step, the scalar penalty with $\lambda=2$ gives $(3,0,0)$ because dropping $-2$ is a tie.

When a dense projection with all entries counted must drop entries and the budget exceeds one, sampled quickselect finds the cutoff magnitude. The selector keeps entries above the cutoff and fills the remaining quota from equal magnitudes in logical column-major order. The SparseIterate selector includes the logical coordinate in its key, so a different active-slot order leaves the tie decision unchanged.

For a symmetric input with counted diagonals, a diagonal costs one budget position, and an off-diagonal pair costs two. The implementation selects and sorts the eligible magnitude prefixes of both classes. It compares the gain from the next pair with the loss from the displaced diagonals, using aligned integer units for the close comparisons. The smallest maximizing pair count resolves a split tie. With free diagonals, the projection copies the diagonal and retains up to $\lfloor s/2\rfloor$ pairs.
