The shared ℓ₁-ball level

One level search serves the projection onto the boundary for IndBallL1 and the clipping proximal maps for NormLinf and GroupNormLinf. The family contracts describe the conditions on use and the accuracy of those maps. See the ball projection, NormLinf range rules, and GroupNormLinf range rules.

Ordinary path

For finite magnitudes $y_i=\lvert x_i\rvert$ and positive radius $a<\sum_i y_i$, the exact level $\tau$ satisfies $g(\tau)=\sum_i (y_i-\tau)_+=a$. Above the level, the projection keeps magnitude $y_i-\tau$ with the input sign. The complementary clip keeps magnitude $\min(y_i,\tau)$ with the input sign.

For $x=(3,-2,1)$ and $a=2$, the two magnitudes above the level give $(3-\tau)+(2-\tau)=2$. The level $3/2$ gives projection $(3/2,-1/2,0)$ and clip $(3/2,-3/2,1)$.

The current selector starts with Condat's candidate pass. If the candidate count grows large, it can hand over to a Michelot pass on all entries. Both routes use the same refinement loop. At a trial level with $k>0$ magnitudes above it, the slope of $g$ on that interval is $-k$. The update adds $(g(\tau)-a)/k$ to the level, with guards for rounding and the next magnitude.

Exceptional branches

If the sum of the magnitudes of finite entries overflows or exceeds half the largest finite value in the working type, callers can read both the magnitudes and the radius in power-of-two units, then rescale the level. Non-finite inputs go through caller-specific endpoint paths. For IndBallL1, a very small radius can make the computed level round to the largest magnitude. The projection has a separate fallback that sorts magnitudes and distributes the radius across gaps. Lowering the level from the $j$th magnitude $b_j$ to the next spends $j(b_j-b_{j+1})$ of the radius. When the remaining radius fits in the current gap, the routine forms the output magnitudes from gaps and shares of that remainder. This avoids subtracting a rounded global level from the largest entries. See the IndBallL1 range rules.
