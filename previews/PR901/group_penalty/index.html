The ℓ₂ and group ℓ₂ norms

Assumes: the ℓ₁ norm guide and grouped variables · You will learn: the Euclidean norm penalty, the group-lasso penalty, their proximal operators, the compact block form and the group optimality screen.

The ℓ₂ norm

NormL2(λ) is $g(x)=\lambda\lVert x\rVert_2$ (the Frobenius norm for a matrix) with $\lambda\ge0$. Its proximal operator shrinks the whole argument, $\max(1-\gamma\lambda/\lVert x\rVert_2,0)\,x$. On a nonempty argument it is the group ℓ₂ norm below with one group 1:length(x) of weight one, and follows the same rules, the exceptional cases included.

using ProximalBase, LinearAlgebra
h = NormL2(0.5)
@assert h([3.0, 4.0]) == 2.5
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 2.0) ≈ (1 - 1 / 5) * [3.0, 4.0]
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 10.0) == [0.0, 0.0]          # ‖x‖ = 5 ≤ γλ
@assert prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 1.0) == prox(GroupNormL2(0.5, [1:2], [1.0]), [3.0, 4.0], 1.0)
prox(h, [3.0, 4.0], 2.0)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.4000000000000004
 3.2

The value takes arrays and the three iterates. On a SymmetricSparseIterate it is the Frobenius norm of the symmetric matrix: each stored off-diagonal coordinate counts twice in the squared norm. On a SparseIterate or a SymmetricSparseIterate it reads only the stored entries, also when a sum of squares that overflows or underflows is recomputed (#808). The proximal operators take arrays of the same size; a BlockIterate or a SparseIterate is read and written by indexing its entries, O(length) in general, as an array. A SymmetricSparseIterate has no NormL2 proximal operator: those calls throw an ArgumentError before anything is written.

The group ℓ₂ norm

GroupNormL2 represents the group-lasso penalty

\[g(x)=\lambda_0\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2,\]

over disjoint groups $G_k$ of linear indices of x. The groups may have any sizes and orders, such as 1:5 or [7, 2, 9]; indices in no group are not penalized. The level $\lambda_0$ and the weights $w_k$ are nonnegative, and the weights default to $w_k=\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}$.

Mathematical formJulia constructorMeaning
$\lambda_0\sum_k\sqrt{\lvert G_k\rvert}\,\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$GroupNormL2(λ0, groups)Default weights
$\lambda_0\sum_k w_k\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2$GroupNormL2(λ0, groups, w)One nonnegative weight per group
$G_k=\{k\}$ for every $k$GroupNormL2(λ0, [[i] for i in 1:p], w)The weighted ℓ₁ norm NormL1(λ0, w)

The constructor keeps its own copies of groups and w. A negative or NaN level or weight, an empty group, a nonpositive index and an index in two groups (or twice in one) throw an ArgumentError; a weight count that differs from the group count throws a DimensionMismatch.

groups = [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3]]          # entry 6 is in no group
g = GroupNormL2(0.4, groups, [1.0, 3.0])
x = [1.0, -2.0, 0.5, 2.0, 0.0, 7.0]
@assert g(x) ≈ 0.4 * (norm(x[[1, 4, 2]]) + 3 * norm(x[[5, 3]]))
g(x)
1.8000000000000003

Proximal operator: group soft thresholding

For a step $\gamma\ge0$, the proximal operator acts on each group separately:

\[\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}(x)_{G_k} =\max\left(1-\frac{\gamma\lambda_0w_k}{\lVert x_{G_k}\rVert_2},0\right)x_{G_k},\]

and leaves the entries in no group unchanged. A group whose norm is at most the threshold $\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ becomes zero; any other group keeps its direction and loses $\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ of its norm.

γ = 0.5
p = prox(g, x, γ)
@assert p[[1, 4, 2]] ≈ (1 - γ * 0.4 / 3) * x[[1, 4, 2]]   # ‖x[G₁]‖ = 3
@assert p[[5, 3]] == [0, 0]                                 # ‖x[G₂]‖ = 0.5 ≤ γ⋅0.4⋅3
@assert p[6] == x[6]
p
6-element Vector{Float64}:

  0.0
  1.8666666666666667
  0.0
  7.0

prox!(y, g, x, γ) writes into y and accepts y === x. value_and_prox! also returns the value at the stored output. The step is a nonnegative scalar: a negative or NaN step throws an ArgumentError before anything is written.

Compact block operations

prox_block!(out, g, u, k, γ) computes the result for one group from a compact trial vector u, whose entries follow the order of groups[k], and writes it into out. It returns out, and out === u is supported. The group index, the buffer lengths, the step and the overlap of out and u are checked before any write. prox_block(g, u, k, γ) allocates the output.

The call does not write to or activate any iterate: u may be a view that reads one, and out must be independent of iterate storage, the caller precondition of the block contract. With a BlockIterate that has the groups of g (for example BlockIterate(p, g)), the caller builds the trial, computes the result in its own buffer and then writes it, which is the step that activates the group:

f = GroupNormL2(0.4, [[1, 4, 2], [5, 3], [6]])
z = BlockIterate(6, f)
u = [3.0, 4.0, 12.0]                        # ‖u‖ = 13
out = prox_block(f, u, 1, 1.0)
@assert out ≈ (1 - 0.4 * sqrt(3) / 13) * u
@assert nactive(z) == 0                     # the pure oracle changed nothing
copyto!(blockview(z, 1), out)
@assert collect(activecoordinates(z)) == [1]
out
3-element Vector{Float64}:
  2.840118386993642
  3.7868245159915226
 11.360473547974568

The block storage guide describes the activation rule of these writes, and the pure block-prox contract the general extension hook.

Optimality screen

For a smooth part h with gradient G = ∇h(x), a zero group $k$ is optimal for h + g when $\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_2\le\lambda_0w_k$. On a BlockIterate, kkt_violation(g, G, x, k) returns $\lVert G_{G_k}\rVert_2-\lambda_0w_k$: a positive value means that zero violates the condition. Group k of the iterate must equal groups[k]. On an array point, kkt_violation(g, G, x, i) takes an entry i: it is the same group screen when the group of i is zero in x, and $\lvert G_i\rvert$ when i is in no group or its group is nonzero. In the group screen, a zero weight gives the coefficient 0 (also with an infinite level), and an infinite weight, or an infinite level with a positive weight, gives -Inf for a finite gradient. Norms and coefficients outside the range are recomputed for finite gradients. When the screened group holds a ±Inf entry and no NaN, the screen is the IEEE $\infty-\lambda_0w_k$, which is NaN when the coefficient is infinite, also when it overflows from finite factors; a NaN entry gives NaN.

G = [0.3, -0.4, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 2.0]
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z, 1) ≈ 0.5 - 0.4 * sqrt(3)
@assert kkt_violation(f, G, z, 3) ≈ 2.0 - 0.4
kkt_violation(f, G, z, 3)
1.6

Exceptional parameters and values

CaseResult for group $k$
$w_k=0$Unpenalized: returned unchanged, NaN and ±Inf included; value zero
$w_k=\infty$, or $\lambda_0=\infty$ with $w_k>0$Fixed at +0 at every nonnegative step, $\gamma=0$ included, also for NaN and ±Inf inputs (#665)
$\lambda_0=0$ or $\gamma=0$, finite parametersThe identity
$\gamma=\infty$, positive finite level and weightFixed at +0
A NaN entry, positive finite level, weight and stepEvery entry of the group becomes NaN
A ±Inf entry and no NaN, positive finite level, weight and stepReturned unchanged: the norm is infinite and the threshold $\gamma\lambda_0w_k$ is finite, also when its floating-point product overflows
$\gamma<0$ or $\gamma=$ NaNArgumentError before any write

At $\gamma=0$ an infinite level is the limit $\gamma\to0^+$ with the function fixed, the projection onto the closure of its domain, as for NormL1(Inf). For finite inputs, norms whose sums of squares overflow or underflow and thresholds outside the floating-point range are recomputed in power-of-two units, so that a representable proximal point is returned accurately. Values recover terms that round below floatmin as NormL1 values do. Values at or above floatmax/2 are recomputed in power-of-two units; within a few eps of the overflow threshold the result may be floatmax or Inf (#904; see the numerical contract).

GroupNormL2 does not apply to a SymmetricSparseIterate (its proximal operators throw an ArgumentError before anything is written), and it has no restrict, no per-group step and no compact block method under Scaled. With a positive finite α, Scaled(GroupNormL2(...), α) multiplies the value by α, computes the array proximal operator with the step αγ, and gives the screen α*kkt_violation(f, g/α, x, k) (test/groupnorml2.jl checks the array prox and the screen). A zero α throws an ArgumentError, and an infinite α has no endpoint rule of its own. The group-lasso example checks the value, the proximal operators, the block operations and the screen against their formulas.
