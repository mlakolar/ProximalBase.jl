# Enclosing the signed ℓ₁ margin

For a stored vector `x` and represented radius `r`, let $S=\sum_i |x_i|$ and $d=S-r$ in exact real arithmetic. Throughout, an available result means finite binary64 endpoints $l\le d\le u$. The result certifies feasibility if $u\le0$ and infeasibility if $l>0$. The violation is bounded by $\max(0,u)$ and the inward deficit by $\max(0,-l)$.

The tested inputs are `Vector{Float32}` and `Vector{Float64}` and same-type positive normal finite radii. The float32-to-float64 conversion and magnitude are exact. Assume standard IEEE 754 round-to-nearest, ties-to-even, gradual underflow, and Julia's ordinary floating-point code generation. The error-free transforms use scalar operations in the written order. Disable flush-to-zero and preserve subnormal operands. The experimental entry points check Julia's zero-subnormal mode. [Julia floating-point modes](https://docs.julialang.org/en/v1/base/numbers/#Base.Rounding.get_zero_subnormals)

## A. One-pass screen

The positive sum uses `_linf_sum` in Float64 for both input types. The SIMD reduction can change the addition tree. Each stored magnitude is a leaf once, and zero initial lanes introduce no error. The rounded path of a leaf has at most $k=\max(0,n-1)$ nontrivial additions for every tree. With unit roundoff $\epsilon_\mathrm{u}=2^{-53}$ and $c=k\epsilon_\mathrm{u}\le 1/4$, write $\gamma=c/(1-c)$. Then $|s-S|\le\gamma S\le \gamma s/(1-\gamma)\le 2cs$ for the computed nonnegative sum $s$. Form $E=\operatorname{nextfloat}(\mathrm{RN}(2cs))$. The factor $2c$ is exact within the depth guard. The next-float step also covers a nonzero product rounded down to zero. The code returns `nothing` when it cannot form a finite enclosure. [Higham, *The Accuracy of Floating Point Summation*, §2](https://nhigham.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/high93s.pdf)

For $m=\mathrm{RN}(s-r)$, the predecessor and successor of $m$ enclose $s-r$. Subtract $E$ from the lower endpoint and add $E$ to the upper endpoint, then move each result one float farther outward. The zero-error cases use an error-free two-term addition and the sign of its exact residual to form the smallest adjacent-float enclosure of that pair. The same proof applies to a native Float32 sum when its own unit roundoff and dimension guard are used. It yields a wider interval of the stored mass.

## B. Screen followed by a signed residual

The experimental query refines when the screen contains elements on both sides of a represented cutoff, counting equality on the lower side. The refinement starts the high word at $-r$ and uses a Knuth `TwoSum` for each magnitude. With high word $h$ and exact local errors $e_i$, $d=h+\sum e_i$. In the same scan compute $z=\mathrm{fl}(\sum e_i)$ and $q=\mathrm{fl}(\sum |e_i|)$. The signed summation error is bounded by $\gamma\sum |e_i|$, and the same inversion for the nonnegative $q$ bounds it by $2cq$. This gives an outward interval for $h+z$ widened by an outward-rounded $2cq$. The radius subtraction is included from the first operation. No extra operand traversal is required for the residual mass. [Ogita, Rump and Oishi, *Accurate Sum and Dot Product*, Eq. (2.4) and Theorem 3.4](https://ogilab.w.waseda.jp/ogita/math/doc/2005_OgRuOi.pdf)

## C. Fixed-order compensated pass

The high word accumulates the mass and each error term comes from `FastTwoSum`, ordering the positive operands by magnitude with one comparison. Decompose the final $h-r$ with `TwoSum` and append its exact low word to both residual reductions. The first mass addition was exact, so there are at most $n$ terms in these residual reductions and the same $k=\max(0,n-1)$ bound applies. Near the boundary, the high word of the final subtraction is already small and the low word still accounts for lost mass. The endpoints include rounding in both the residual enclosure and the final addition. [Ogita, Rump and Oishi, §3–4](https://ogilab.w.waseda.jp/ogita/math/doc/2005_OgRuOi.pdf)

These addition and subtraction transforms allow gradual underflow. With inputs that are binary64 floats, a sum or difference in the subnormal range is a multiple of the least subnormal spacing and exact. The error-radius multiplication can round in that range and the outward step covers its absolute rounding. A nonfinite high word, residual accumulator or endpoint makes the result unavailable. The study makes no claim of range recovery or of a definite result after cancellation. Subnormal, overflow, zero-radius, infinite-radius, and NaN probes are tabulated separately.

## Independent reference

The exact reference converts each stored coefficient and radius separately to BigFloat and uses 2304 bits for the dyadic sum and difference. Each finite binary64 number is an integer multiple of $2^{-1074}$ with integer magnitude below $2^{2098}$. The tested sums have fewer than 4096 terms, so the reference fits within the precision even with the full exponent span. The reference is independent of the accumulation order and rounding of the three prototypes. The validator asserts containment and verifies definite classifications for every finite reported interval. BigFloat is confined to validation and fixture construction.
